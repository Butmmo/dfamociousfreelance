# BEF Institutional Identity & Participant Framework

Working reference: BEF Constitution (Restated). The institutional identity, participant framework, and discipline architecture below are load-bearing across every initiative, not specific to any one of them.

**Superseded, do not use:** the constitution's 60/40 earnings split (beneficiary 60%, institution 40%) and its $1,500 qualified-month threshold. Current figures are DFY's 20% and $2,000 — see `digital-systems-engineering`'s `references/pricing-and-dfy.md`.

## Participant status: covenant, not just a label

Entry into Intern or Beneficiary status (or the equivalent tier in any initiative) requires executing a **Formal Covenant** binding the participant to the Foundation's principles, disciplinary code, and long-term vision — not merely a status change on completing a course. Advancement between tiers is explicitly merit-based and discretionary, contingent on documented programmatic and disciplinary fulfilment (including a clean record of serious offences), never automatic on time served.

Failure to maintain covenant standards — including breaches of loyalty or financial discipline — results in loss of achieved status and exit from the ecosystem.

**Advancement is specific, not just "merit-based":** demonstrated competence in required digital skills, zero Level 3 or Level 4 offences, and completion of required financial/savings milestones — not a vague judgment call.

## Governance sits above BEF, not inside it

The Founder, Chancellor, DFG Elder Council, and DFG Board of Stewards/CEO are DFG-level authorities, not BEF's own — full detail lives in `dfg`, not here, and shouldn't be re-paraphrased in this file. What matters at BEF's own level: BEF Officers and Governors are bound by BEF's Conflict of Interest policy below, which is distinct from (and more operational than) DFG's own voting-recusal rule — both apply, neither replaces the other.

## Conflict of interest: a real, existing policy, not something to build from scratch

Every Officer, Governor, or person holding a position of trust or financial authority executes an **annual, formal Conflict of Interest Declaration**. Real teeth, not a formality: mandatory recusal from any deliberation, approval, vendor selection, donor negotiation, or discipline case where impartiality is compromised; gifts/hospitality that could influence judgment are prohibited, with permitted tokens declared and logged; and **any related-party transaction requires heightened review, written justification, competitive comparison where practicable, and approval by the appropriate oversight authority.** Undisclosed conflicts or self-dealing are at minimum a Level 3 offence; fraud or kickbacks are Gross Misconduct.

**This is the exact mechanism that should govern the BEF-DBI reimbursement.** The $1,000-per-completed-NBC-intern payment (`dbi`'s `SKILL.md`) is a related-party transaction between two entities under common institutional origin — it should go through this review process formally, not operate as an informal understanding, however well-intentioned.

## Safeguarding of minors and vulnerable persons

A non-negotiable, zero-tolerance institutional priority, in physical and digital environments alike. Any employee, officer, or affiliate who knows or reasonably suspects abuse, harm, or neglect of a minor or vulnerable person has an **absolute, mandatory obligation** to report immediately to institutional authority and, where legally required, external law enforcement. Failure to report is itself a Level 4 Gross Misconduct offence — not a lesser failure than the underlying harm.

## Treasury principles, briefly — the framing the Pocket System sits under

Three principles govern all institutional funds: **Stewardship** (assets treated as a sacred trust, decisions prioritizing institutional longevity and beneficiary impact over convenience), **Separation** (restricted, unrestricted, and operational funds held in genuinely separate accounts — commingling is a Level 4 offence), and **Traceability** (every transaction auditable from source to expenditure). The Pocket System below is the individual-level expression of the same discipline the institution holds itself to.

## Independent contractor status (legally important, easy to overlook)

Participation in any BEF program, training phase, or activity does **not** create an employment relationship. Both the Foundation and the participant expressly disclaim employment; the participant carries independent liability and compliance responsibility. State this plainly whenever a participant's relationship to BEF or any of its initiatives is discussed — it is a real legal distinction, not boilerplate. This applies across NBO's NBC → NBS → BPN progression and should apply identically inside any future initiative.

## The Pocket System

Mandatory income segregation: personal income and revenue divided into defined, audited categories (PV Use, Savings, Investment, Personal Use). Ratified as the core mechanism for enforcing delayed gratification and economic responsibility, with periodic audit rights and non-compliance treated as a financial discipline violation. Administered at the initiative level in practice, under BEF's mandate.

**Current relevance inside NBO specifically:** BPN requires beneficiaries to fund ongoing Neolife product purchases (up to 300 PPV, 250 advised) out of their own income. The Pocket System's "PV Use" category is the existing, purpose-built answer to that — teach it as the mechanism for funding BPN participation sustainably, rather than leaving beneficiaries to improvise from general income.

## Discipline: four levels, with fair warning

- **Level 1 (Minor):** isolated operational lapses. Correction-focused.
- **Level 2 (Moderate Operational):** repeated Level 1 failures (three or more interventions), impact on short-term team stability, violation of non-financial internal rules. Formal caution.
- **Level 3 (Serious Institutional):** material breaches of financial discipline, sustained insubordination, public conduct damaging the Foundation's reputation, persistent systemic non-compliance.
- **Level 4 (Gross Misconduct):** unauthorized fund diversion, fraud, legal violations, forgery, theft of institutional IP, systemic data breach, premeditated betrayal.

**The fair-warning principle is the part that matters most:** no sanction may be levied for an offence not explicitly defined in advance, and every breach must map to a defined violation. Disciplinary action is framed as objective measurement against a published standard, not personal judgment.

**Before any of the four levels apply at all, a real classification question comes first: is this actually misconduct, or is it a good-faith failure?** Three categories, and only the third reaches the discipline ladder above:

- **Acceptable failure** — a market assumption didn't hold, a pilot underperformed for external reasons, documented due diligence was done and things still didn't work. Response: review, documented correction, active support. **No sanctions apply at all.**
- **Correctable failure** — systems poorly implemented, weak reporting, a skills gap relative to the responsibility held. Response: mandatory retraining, temporary oversight, a monitored improvement plan — still not the discipline ladder.
- **Disciplinary failure** — persistent insubordination, ignored repeated warnings, demonstrable financial mismanagement, irresponsible use of authority. Only this category engages Levels 1-4 above.

This isn't a separate idea from the DFY-period reframing already built into `digital-systems-engineering` — it's the constitutional basis for it. A beneficiary with several non-qualified DFY months hasn't necessarily failed anything sanctionable; absent evidence of the third category, it's presumptively acceptable or correctable failure, and the response should look like the first two categories, not the fourth. Apply this classification test *before* reaching for the discipline ladder, in any initiative, not just during DFY specifically — this should govern discipline in any initiative, not just NBO.

## Catholic identity, with freedom of conscience

BEF operates under DFG's Catholic institutional alignment — visible Catholic culture in official program spaces, regular institutional Mass, and an Office of Catholic Life oversight interface. Held alongside an explicit commitment that persons of any faith are received with respect and justice, with no coercion of conscience. Both halves are constitutional; state both together, never one without the other.

## The Panel

A standing body responsible for vetting, reviewing, and enabling participant-led startups — the constitutional precursor to BEF's still-developing SUC business-funding initiative, and the governance layer for SUC entry decisions generally.

**Vetting criteria:** alignment with BEF's long-term nation-building vision, compliance with DFG's legal and governance framework, and demonstrated financial sustainability and job-creation capacity.

**The single most important provision — state it every time The Panel comes up:** vetting is an endorsement of institutional readiness, **not** a guarantee of funding. A student who passes review has not been promised money.

**Keep it minimal.** The original constitution implies full committee apparatus; at DBI's current scale, The Panel should be two or three people convening when there's an actual decision. Encode the function and the vetting-≠-funding rule; don't build ceremony that can't be staffed. Its value is that SUC entry and equity decisions get a named body with published criteria rather than undocumented founder discretion — which matters precisely because DBI holds equity in the outcome and is therefore not a neutral party.

## An expansion hierarchy exists on paper, not yet in practice

The constitution defines a multi-tier structure beyond NBA/NBS/NBX — Officers advancing through Chief, Leader, National Director, up to a President role, plus an Overseer function — built for a multi-Centre, multi-region operation with a defined process for spinning up new Centres. This is real, well-designed content, not something to discard. But it's built for a scale the ecosystem hasn't reached yet, consistent with the "seminal stage" framing elsewhere: implementing it now would be ceremony without the operation to justify it. Worth returning to once NBO (or another initiative) genuinely operates across multiple locations, not before.

The constitution also mandates **shared services** across every DFG subsidiary and initiative — centralized legal/compliance, treasury and audit, data protection infrastructure, and HR/personnel vetting — with exemptions only by unanimous Elder Council vote. Worth having on record as the design intent even before it's fully built out: new initiatives (BSA, BLI) shouldn't each build their own version of these functions from scratch.
