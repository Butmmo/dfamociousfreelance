# Ecosystem Relevance

Covers stage 8 of The Start-up Catalyst.

## Two different games, gated by resources

"Cement its relevance in its ecosystem" splits into two genuinely different strategies depending on how resourced the product already is. Don't apply the bigger one before the smaller one is won.

### Year 1-2, solo-appropriate: niche dominance

Own one small, specific segment completely rather than reaching broadly across a big one. The evidence for this is consistent and somewhat counterintuitive: a tight, engaged audience in the thousands reliably outperforms a loose, disengaged audience an order of magnitude larger. A documented pattern among solo builders: real traction arrives not from broadening the pitch, but from narrowing hard into one previously-overlooked segment and building specifically for it — sometimes after a period of broad, unfocused effort that wasn't working.

**Practical application:** before attempting to define a new category, make sure the product is the obvious, complete answer within one specific, narrow segment first. This is the honest, resource-appropriate version of ecosystem relevance for a Year 1-2 solo operator.

### Year 3+, resourced: category design

Category design means owning the *definition* of a problem rather than competing on features within someone else's existing category — and it's credited with letting the resulting "category kings" capture a large majority of their category's total economic value, far more than a strong #2 or #3 competitor in the same space captures.

This works through three layers built together, not separately: how the company itself is designed around the category it's claiming, how the category's own language and standards get defined and taught to the market, and how real reference customers and partners get activated as visible proof that the category is real and the company leads it.

**The honest caveat:** this framework assumes a leadership team and real PR/market-education resources — it was built by and for venture-backed companies with a CEO and a team behind the category push, not a solo operator working alone. Treat category design as the stage 8 upgrade a product graduates into once niche dominance has produced enough traction, revenue, or team to support it — not as a Year 1 starting move. Recommending full category design to a solo, pre-traction builder is a common and costly mismatch; recommend niche dominance instead and revisit category design once the resource picture has changed.
