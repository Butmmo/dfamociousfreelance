# Valuation: Course, Membership, Mastermind & License

Covers stage 9 of The Start-up Catalyst for the four product types that aren't software businesses in the venture sense. These get valued in earnings terms, not revenue-multiple terms — a genuinely different regime from `valuation-saas.md`, not a smaller version of the same one.

**Recency note:** figures below reflect research gathered in 2026; revisit periodically.

## Course & Membership: Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE)

Small, owner-operated businesses — which is what a Course or Membership business is, even a successful one — typically sell for **2-4x SDE**, averaging around 2.5x across industries. SDE, not revenue: this is earnings after real costs, adjusted to add back the owner's own compensation and personal expenses run through the business.

**The one lever that moves this multiple meaningfully:** recurring revenue. Businesses with contracts, subscriptions, or reliable repeat customers reduce a buyer's risk of post-sale revenue decline, and consistently sell at the higher end of their industry's range. This is the concrete, financial argument for pairing a Course with a Membership continuity tier rather than selling it as a pure one-off — it's not just a marketing upsell, it's a direct valuation lever.

**Calculating it:** start from the business's net profit, then add back whatever the owner currently pays themselves (a buyer isn't paying that salary — they're buying the cash flow), any personal expenses run through the business (phone plan, vehicle, a "conference trip" that was really a vacation), one-time costs that won't recur (a single launch ad spend, a one-off legal fee), and any interest on debt. What's left is SDE — multiply by 2-4x for a ballpark valuation.

## Business-in-a-Box License: franchise/royalty math

This is a third, distinct regime — value the licensor and the licensee separately, because they're different assets:

- **The licensor (the student, if they're the one licensing out the system):** valued on a multiple of the royalty income stream, typically **4-6x EBITDA** on a weighted-average basis, ranging 3x to 8x+ depending on brand strength, the royalty structure itself, unit economics across licensees, and how territory rights are structured. Per `SKILL.md`'s ownership section, the student stays licensor of record here — this valuation is the student's business, with DBI's default 25% sitting underneath as equity, not a separate DBI-held asset.
- **The licensee (whoever buys a license):** their individual unit is valued on its own owner earnings, typically **2.5-3.5x** for a single unit, varying meaningfully by category.

**The tension a student needs to understand before pricing a royalty:** the fee structure that makes the licensor's income stream valuable is the same structure that compresses the licensee's own profitability. A typical royalty (roughly 4-6% of gross revenue) plus a marketing fund contribution (roughly 2-4%) takes something like 6-10% of a licensee's revenue off the top before any profit — which can reduce a licensee's cash earnings by 15-25%. Price the royalty too aggressively and the license network becomes unattractive to join, which eventually caps the licensor's own valuation too, since a licensor is only as valuable as the network of licensees willing to keep paying in. Structure the royalty deliberately, with the licensee's real margin in mind — not just what maximizes near-term licensor revenue.

## What this means for Structure and Optimize

Because SDE rewards recurring revenue and franchise value depends on a healthy licensee network, packaging (see `design-and-structure.md`) should build in a renewal, subscription, or repeat-purchase component wherever the product legitimately supports one — and any royalty or fee structure should be pressure-tested against the licensee's actual resulting margin before it's finalized, not set purely to maximize the licensor's take.
