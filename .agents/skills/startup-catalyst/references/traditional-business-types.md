## The three settings, defined

**Setting** describes where the business's home base sits — not where its customers are — independent of **type**, which describes the business model. Home base is what decides the setting: an export business based in Lagos selling to European buyers is still a *Developing*-setting business, even though its customers are abroad (see Type 7's Developing list, which includes international buyers as a demographic *within* that setting). Even one type (Import/Export) takes three genuinely different shapes depending on setting; don't treat "international" as redundant with a cross-border-flavored type, and don't confuse "sells internationally" with the International setting — that setting is reserved for operations genuinely split across two or more countries, not just sales reaching abroad from one home base.

- **Developed country** — home base is a single developed economy. High purchasing power, formal/regulated channels, high labor costs, often-saturated competition, digital-payment-first infrastructure.
- **Developing country** — home base is a single developing economy, regardless of where customers are. Large informal-economy share, cash/mobile-money-heavy payments, real infrastructure gaps that themselves create opportunity, relationship/trust-based commerce, younger/faster-growing demand.
- **International / cross-border** — not anchored to one home market; operations themselves (sourcing, processing, or delivery) are genuinely split across two or more countries, not just sales reaching abroad. Forex management, cross-border logistics and customs, diaspora-linked demand, multi-jurisdiction complexity.

## The cross-cutting rule for developing-country settings

Real estate's title-verification gap, export's documented trust gap between foreign buyers and local suppliers, and retail's screenshot-and-bank-alert trust-building are the same finding three times over: **in a developing-country setting, systemized verification and trust infrastructure is the differentiator — not price, not reach.** The competition is overwhelmingly informal, not incompetent. A DBI grad's edge is being the one operator who documents, verifies, and reports properly. Apply this by default; look for what "verified" or "systemized" means in the specific type before reaching for price or marketing as the differentiator.

## 1. Systemized Local Service Business
Home/trade/care services, owned rather than serviced for a client — CRM and workflow discipline is the actual product.

**Developed:** Home services exceed $500B annually in the US alone; senior care is the fastest-growing segment. Reliability and no-show avoidance are the sale.
- Dual-income homeowners wanting hassle-free scheduling
- Aging-in-place seniors and their adult children
- Property managers / multi-unit landlords (recurring B2B)
- New/relocating homeowners — high urgency, strong referrals
- Small commercial property owners

**Developing:** Informal service channels dominate across every income group; basic reliability is a rare, real differentiator.
- Middle-class households in gated estates paying for reliability
- Landlords/estate managers of multi-unit compounds
- Diaspora-owned properties needing remote-managed upkeep
- Small businesses needing facility upkeep
- New estate developments — become the developer's default vendor
- Aging parents whose adult children have relocated (locally or diaspora) — the direct extension of CareBridge's own core market, now owned locally instead of served as an automation client

**International:** Diaspora property care, no single home base. 170M+ African diaspora hold an estimated $1.4T in annual purchasing power.
- Diaspora property owners paying in hard currency, needing remote trust/reporting
- Diaspora investment cooperatives managing pooled property
- Repatriating families needing trustworthy local setup
- Cross-border landlords wanting one relationship across countries
- NGOs/international orgs with local facility needs

## 2. Retail & Consumer Trading
Buy-sell margin model, physical or hybrid.

**Developed:** TikTok Shop carries 70M+ products; independent sellers are already a third of its sales. Hybrid browse-online/buy-in-store shoppers convert 25-30% better.
- Short-form-video-driven impulse buyers
- BOPIS shoppers wanting hybrid convenience
- Value-conscious "dupe"/nostalgia shoppers
- Community-driven buyers loyal to creator-validated small brands
- Loyalty/subscription shoppers wanting curated repeat relationships

**Developing:** The typical Nigerian online store is a WhatsApp conversation — post on Instagram/TikTok, negotiate and pay in DMs. Trust proof (screenshots, payment confirmations) is part of the product.
- WhatsApp-first buyers expecting relationship-based negotiation
- Trust-skeptical first-time online buyers needing visible proof
- Niche-specific buyers where systemized selling stands out from thousands of informal competitors
- Repeat customers built through consistent, structured service
- Informal-market buyers migratable to a trackable, data-driven relationship

**International:** Diaspora/cross-border retail, AfCFTA lowering regional friction.
- Diaspora buyers wanting authentic home-country products shipped abroad
- Regional buyers purchasing across neighboring countries via one platform
- International boutiques wanting verified African-made goods
- Diaspora gift-givers sending goods to family at home
- Ethically-conscious global buyers wanting traceable, small-producer-linked goods

## 3. Real Estate & Property
Brokerage-centered; property management and development are natural extensions once established.

**Developed:** Post-NAR-settlement commission compression (~5.44% average combined) is pushing brokerages toward efficiency and top-performer consolidation; flat-fee models are real alternatives now.
- First-time buyers navigating the newly-unbundled commission negotiation
- Sellers comparing flat-fee vs. traditional models
- Investor/landlord clients building rental portfolios
- Relocating/remote-work buyers expecting a concierge process
- Small commercial property buyers/tenants

**Developing:** Land title costs run 100-200% of property value (vs. ~0.5% in the US) in a largely unregulated agency market. Title verification is the product, not a compliance detail.
- First-time local buyers needing genuine title verification
- Landlords in the informal rental market wanting professional management
- Land/plot investors in emerging growth corridors
- Developers needing a competent sales partner for off-plan units
- Corporate/institutional tenants seeking properly-documented space

**International (diaspora real estate):** Naira depreciation gives hard-currency buyers a real discount; formal channels now exist (NRNIA, FMBN Diaspora Mortgage at 9%/30% equity) alongside well-documented fraud risk (fake titles, double sales).
- Family-use + rental buyers (home for visits, income between visits)
- Growth-corridor investors accepting development risk for appreciation
- Buyers who need remote verification/escrow as the core product
- Repatriating diaspora planning permanent return
- Formal-financing users — a distinct, more bankable segment than cash buyers

## 4. Food, Beverage & Hospitality
High-local-texture, experience-delivered.

**Developed:** A $1.55T industry where 42% of operators were still unprofitable last year. Costs for eating out have risen roughly double grocery costs since 2024. The real differentiator is what one report calls the "Hospitality Gap" — human connection, not tech.
- Value-conscious households trading delivery for pickup
- Experience-seeking diners wanting genuine hospitality, not just food
- Nostalgia/comfort/"flavor escapism" seekers
- Fellow operators needing unified ordering/data tech
- Diners trading up from QSR to casual, now seen as the better value

**Developing:** An $8.4B market growing 7.6% annually; fusion cuisine is the fastest-growing segment. Government is actively building food into tourism infrastructure.
- Time-pressed urban professionals wanting traditional food in a modern, fast format
- Fusion-seeking younger, cosmopolitan diners
- Diners across the full range from fine dining to casual
- Corporate/event catering clients — weddings and functions
- Festival/exhibitor opportunities tied to government-backed food-tourism events

**International (diaspora hospitality):** Lagos's 2024 "Detty December" season alone generated $71.6M across tourism, hospitality, and entertainment, growing further in 2025. Organized diaspora tours exist specifically to counter negative perceptions absorbed abroad.
- Seasonal diaspora visitors wanting hospitality, entertainment, and short-let bundled
- Diaspora tour groups wanting curated, guided experiences
- First-time diaspora/foreign visitors needing a systemized, reassuring experience
- Hotels/short-lets themselves, as a B2B supply-side segment tied to the season
- Diaspora event planners needing one reliable local partner

## 5. Agro-Processing & Light Manufacturing
Raw/commodity input → value-added output.

**Developed:** Skip the heavy-reshoring headlines (chip fabs, not a solo-operator lane). The real reachable trend is small-batch, DTC-native manufacturing — brands that mostly never left, or were built domestic from the start. Real "Made in USA" preference exists and is measurable.
- "Made in USA"-conscious premium consumers
- DTC brand builders wanting a small-batch domestic co-manufacturing partner
- Buyers of technical/niche textiles specifically
- Regional retailers wanting exclusive small-batch lines
- Institutional buyers with domestic-sourcing/ESG requirements

**Developing:** Government policy is actively forcing this transition — e.g. Nigeria's raw shea export ban, extended specifically to force processing over raw export, explicitly framed as a template for cashew, sesame, and other commodities.
- Local cosmetics/personal-care brands needing processed (not raw) shea butter
- Confectionery/food manufacturers needing processed cocoa over raw beans
- Export buyers specifically seeking processed goods for duty-free treatment
- Retail chains wanting packaged, branded versions of commodities usually sold loose
- Buyers connected to government value-addition programs

**International (contract manufacturing across borders):** Raw material sourced in one country, processed in another, sold in a third.
- Global beauty/cosmetics brands wanting African-origin ingredients processed to export spec
- Diaspora-founded food/beauty brands sourcing and processing at home, selling abroad
- Contract co-packers serving multiple international clients from one base
- Ethical-sourcing/fair-trade buyers needing a verified, traceable partner
- Regional processors sourcing across neighboring countries under one export brand

## 6. Franchise or Business Acquisition
Buy into a proven system rather than build one from zero. Ownership note: this type is the one exception to DBI's default 25% equity stake (`SKILL.md`) — a self-financed acquisition owes DBI nothing, since DBI contributed no capital to that specific deal; only DBI-financed acquisitions carry equity, scaled to what was actually contributed.

**Developed:** Buyers are more sophisticated — real growth in MBA/search-fund/Entrepreneurship-Through-Acquisition activity — while private equity increasingly buys franchisors outright, treating franchising as a scalable asset class.
- MBA/search-fund buyers running formal ETA processes
- First-time buyers targeting resilient, need-based categories (home services, senior care, child services)
- Multi-unit "professional franchisee" operators consolidating across brands
- Private equity/family offices acquiring franchisor systems outright
- Retiring independent owners seeking a buyer, often via SBA-backed financing

**Developing:** Not a mature buy/sell marketplace — succession planning explains roughly half the variance in whether a Nigerian SME survives its founder's exit, and informal family succession (often unplanned) is the dominant transfer model. The opportunity looks like stepping into a structural gap, not browsing a listings site.
- First-generation owners nearing retirement with no succession plan
- Family businesses where the next generation doesn't want to take over
- Structurally sound but undercapitalized businesses needing systemization plus capital — often a partial/earn-in stake, not a full buyout
- Owners wanting to formalize an informal business as part of the transfer
- Local operators seeking sub-franchisee rights under an existing master franchisee

**International (master franchise):** A master franchisee gets exclusive rights to develop — and sub-franchise — an entire territory, paying an upfront fee plus a share of sub-franchisee revenue. Real risk: this carries an ongoing development schedule a straight acquisition doesn't; miss it and both the territory and the (non-refundable) fee can be lost. Documented, transferable operating systems close these deals faster — directly DBI's own core skill.
- Master franchisees securing territory rights to bring an international brand into Nigeria/West Africa
- Nigerian/African brand owners packaging their own concept for outward expansion
- Sub-franchisees operating individual units under an existing master franchisee
- Investors evaluating opportunities through a documentation/systemization lens specifically
- Diaspora investors bringing a "taste of home" brand into their country of residence, or the reverse

## 7. Import/Export & Cross-Border Trade
Logistics- and forex-based margin.

**Developed:** 2026 is a genuinely bad year for the classic "import cheap from China, resell" model — the sub-$800 duty-free exemption is gone, some categories carry 145%+ total duty, and new Section 301 duties layer on top. Nearshoring to Mexico/USMCA is a real, current mitigation — the tariff environment itself is the opportunity for an operator who scouts around it.
- Importers deliberately sourcing in low-tariff-exposure categories
- Brands actively shifting sourcing from China to Mexico/USMCA-eligible suppliers
- Small sellers needing tariff-classification/compliance help — a real, underserved gap
- Boutique/curated importers who can absorb duty costs into premium positioning
- Domestic retailers wanting a reliable sourcing partner instead of managing it themselves

**Developing:** Cocoa, sesame, soya, and cashew together generate ~90% of Nigeria's agricultural export earnings. A small exporter shipping 10 tonnes of cashew can gross ₦25-30M. AGOA's duty-free window closes December 31, 2026 — a real deadline, not a vague one.
- Smallholder farmer cooperatives needing an export-ready aggregator
- International B2B buyers wanting a verified, documentation-complete supplier relationship
- Local processors moving up the value chain ahead of policy incentives
- Diaspora-linked buyers wanting authentic food/beauty products shipped abroad
- Anyone needing to move before the AGOA window closes

**International (multi-country trade):** No single home base; sourcing, processing, and selling deliberately split across countries. AfCFTA is steadily lowering intra-African trade barriers.
- Diaspora-run platforms connecting home-country producers to diaspora demand abroad
- Multi-country agro-traders aggregating volume/quality across 2+ countries for one contract
- Global specialty brands sourcing African-origin ingredients for processing/branding elsewhere
- Cross-border logistics/customs-clearing intermediaries — the friction itself is the business
- Trade-finance-linked buyers needing payment-guarantee structures with unfamiliar overseas suppliers
