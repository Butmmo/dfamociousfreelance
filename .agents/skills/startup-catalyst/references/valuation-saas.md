# Valuation: SaaS-Shaped Products

Covers stage 9 of The Start-up Catalyst, for Micro-SaaS Tools and any subscription-based Data Product. For Course, Membership, Mastermind, and License, use `valuation-sde-and-licensing.md` instead.

**Recency note:** the figures below reflect research gathered in 2026. Valuation multiples move with market conditions — treat the bands as directional, revisit them periodically, and don't quote them to a student as fixed truth.

## The headline mechanic: revenue quality beats revenue size

The single most load-bearing metric is **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** — the percentage of revenue retained and expanded from existing customers, excluding new customer acquisition. It's frequently described as the definitive SaaS metric of this era, and the multiple impact is dramatic and non-linear:

| NRR band | Typical revenue multiple impact |
|---|---|
| Below 90% | ~1.2x — treated as a red flag |
| 100-110% | ~6x |
| Above 120% | 8x+ |

Small improvements above the 110% line produce disproportionately large valuation jumps — this is worth teaching explicitly, because it means a founder's highest-leverage lever is often reducing churn and growing existing accounts, not just adding new logos.

## ARR multiple bands by scale (directional)

| ARR stage | Typical revenue multiple |
|---|---|
| Under $1M ARR | 2.5-4x |
| $1-5M ARR | 4-6x |
| $5-20M ARR | 5-8x |
| $20M+ ARR, category-leading | 7-10x+ |

## The other mechanics worth teaching directly

- **Gross Revenue Retention (GRR)** — retention excluding expansion, i.e. how much would remain even with zero upsells; a healthy target sits around 90%+.
- **CAC payback period** — how many months of gross margin it takes to recover the cost of acquiring a customer; under 18-24 months is the healthy range.
- **Gross margin** — typically expected in the 70-85% range for a real software business; a lower margin suggests the product has more services/labor mixed into its delivery than a pure software product should.
- **Rule of 40** — growth rate percentage plus profit margin percentage should sum to 40 or more; this is the standard shorthand for "is this a healthy growth-stage business" regardless of which side (growth or profitability) is carrying the number.

## A live wrinkle: the AI-native premium, and its skepticism

Products described as AI-native are currently commanding significant valuation premiums over traditional SaaS in some reporting — but there's real, active scrutiny of "AI-washing" (products or claims that overstate genuine AI-native architecture to capture the premium), including real deals falling through over exactly this concern. Don't coach a student to lean on AI-native positioning without the underlying architecture actually earning it — this is a live risk area, not a free multiple.

## What this means for Structure and Optimize

Because NRR and CAC payback are the real levers, packaging (see `design-and-structure.md`) should favor subscription/recurring structures over one-off sales from day one, and the build/optimize loop should treat churn signals and onboarding friction as valuation-relevant, not just UX-relevant.
