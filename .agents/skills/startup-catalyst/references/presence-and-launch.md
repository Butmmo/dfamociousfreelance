# Presence, Pre-sell, Launch & Content

Covers stages 5-7 of The Start-up Catalyst.

## Presence: build-in-public is the 2026 default

For a solo or small-team builder, sharing the real, unpolished process — not staging a big reveal — is currently the dominant content strategy for pre-launch audience building, and it does double duty as both stage 5 (presence) and stage 7 (content/authority), since it's the same content engine sustained past launch rather than two separate efforts.

**A workable content mix:** roughly 40% real insight/lessons, 30% behind-the-scenes process, 20% opinion/point of view, 10% direct launch/promotional content. Skewing much higher on the promotional slice reads as advertising rather than presence-building.

**The realistic time cost:** a few hours a week, content repurposed one piece into many formats, compounding into real acquisition-cost advantages over roughly 12-18 months — this is a compounding asset, not a launch-week tactic, and should be budgeted as an ongoing stage-7 commitment from the start, not something that stops once stage 6 (Launch) is done.

**Channel set for a solo builder** (adapt per product, don't run all seven at once): build-in-public posts on a primary platform, deep engagement in one or two niche communities where the target buyer already gathers, direct outreach to warm prospects, longer-form platform-native writing, an email list, light SEO, and a single well-prepared community/launch-day event rather than a broad multi-platform blast.

## Pre-sell: real scarcity, not staged urgency

Tiered or descending discount structures work because they convert an uncertain "should I buy this" decision into a "when do I commit" decision — an early cohort gets the deepest discount, a slightly later cohort gets a smaller one, and the price settles at full rate after that.

**The one requirement that makes this legitimate rather than manipulative:** the scarcity has to be real — a genuinely defined cohort size, or a real deadline that actually closes. A discount without a real constraint behind it reads as desperation once buyers notice the "limited time" offer that's always running.

**This mechanism has a hard boundary — see `the-fork.md`.** Discounting of any kind is a stage 5-9 tool. It must be fully retired before a product attempts stage 10's luxury lane; the two are structurally incompatible.

## Launch: format follows product type, not a universal template

There is no single launch-day template. Timeline and channel mix should be built around what the product actually is:

- **SaaS-shaped products** run longer runways (on the order of 12 weeks) with a smaller, qualified waitlist, sustained content on a professional-networking platform, and a single concentrated launch-day event (a community or directory launch) as the capstone.
- **Consumer or high-volume products** (Course, Membership, Data Product) typically run a larger waitlist and lean more on creator/community seeding than on a single professional-network push.
- **High-touch, low-volume products** (Mastermind, License) don't need a mass "launch day" at all — a direct, warm, relationship-led rollout to a pre-identified list fits the product better than a public campaign.

Pick the format from this list based on the specific product being launched — don't default to whichever launch style is most familiar if it doesn't match the product's actual buying pattern.
