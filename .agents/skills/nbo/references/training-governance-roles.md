# NBO Training Governance Roles

Inherited from BEF's Constitution (Art. XXX), updated for NBO's current operating reality and for the fact that DBI is now building software that handles part of what these offices originally did by hand.

## The three offices, and why they're separate

- **NBA (Next Blazers Admin)** — command authority and strategic oversight over NBC training operations: discipline enforcement, curriculum standards, and authoritative policy interpretation within NBC.
- **NBS (Next Blazers Secretary)** — administration and record custody: participant progress tracking, fine schedules, compliance documents. Official Custodian of Records for NBC, responsible for upholding the Principle of Traceability.
- **NBX (Next Blazers Officer)** — tactical execution and the direct mentorship interface: daily operational execution, local compliance monitoring, communication of training mandates to Interns and Beneficiaries. **Explicitly non-policy-setting** — NBX mentors and monitors, but doesn't decide policy.

The separation is the design, not an artifact of org size. The mentor (NBX) is deliberately not the record-keeper (NBS) and not the policy authority (NBA), so nobody both sets the standard and certifies their own mentee against it.

## NBX and the NBO sponsor are the same function

NBO's sponsor — the person who invited a participant in, who runs the check-ins formalised in BPS Assignment 7 — is performing NBX's mentorship mandate under a different name from a different era. Treat them as one role: **the sponsor is the participant's NBX.** This matters practically, because it means the sponsor inherits NBX's constitutional boundaries: mentor and monitor, don't set policy, don't be the one who records compliance for your own mentee.

This is also the function with the strongest evidence behind it (structured goal-setting in a first mentoring session correlates with roughly 3x higher session completion), which makes it the **last** function to automate and the thing software should be freeing human time up for — not replacing.

## What software replaces, and what it must not

NBS's mandate bundles three different kinds of work. They don't automate equally:

**1. Clerical record-keeping — automate fully.** Timestamping submissions, archiving to the registry, tracking progress and fine schedules, generating compliance reports. Software is more accurate, always available, immune to favouritism, and produces exactly the audit trail the Principle of Traceability requires. Build this into the app; don't staff it.

**2. Judgment calls that look clerical — surface in software, decide by human.** Was a late submission a legitimate extension? Does a pattern of near-misses mean a struggling participant or a gaming one? Should a fine be waived? The dangerous failure mode is an app that auto-applies sanctions with no appeal path — that quietly breaks the fair-warning principle inherited from the Internal Code of Discipline, because a participant sanctioned by a system with no human review has no real due process. **Any automated sanction needs a named human in the appeal path.**

**3. Noticing what isn't in the data — human only.** The participant whose scores look fine but who's gone quiet; the sponsor relationship that's quietly broken down. Software can flag anomalies; it can't interpret them.

Net effect: NBS may not need to be a dedicated hire at current scale — automate the clerical layer and keep a named human accountable for the exception queue.

## Employment status: where the line falls

Participants (NBRs, Interns, Beneficiaries) hold **independent-contractor, non-employment status** under BEF Art. 1.4. Governance roles — NBA, NBS, NBX where formalised, and DSE's Rep and Auditor — are **employed, salaried positions** (or genuine industrial-training internships on a path to assimilation).

**Crossing from Beneficiary into a governance role is a change in legal relationship, not just a promotion.** State this explicitly when someone progresses; don't let it be discovered later.
