# Next Blazers College (NBC) — Curriculum Detail

The 40-day-plus, five-stage prep program preceding DSE. Total core duration is **58 days** (approximately two months), plus a 30-day self-development tail that extends past the core (see stage 5).

## Stage timing at a glance

| # | Stage | Duration | Title held |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Onboarding | 10 days | NBR |
| 2 | Blazer Productivity Scheme (BPS) Training | 10 days | NBR |
| 3 | BEF Exposition Course ("Who We Are") | 7 days | NBR |
| 4 | Sales and Marketing | 21 days | Intern |
| 5 | DBI Introductory Course | 10 days | Intern |

**Total: 58 days.** See "Program structure" below for how pace is enforced — a cohort design has replaced the earlier fee-based approach.

**Titling:** stages 1-3 = NBR (Next Blazers Recruit), stages 4-6 = Intern. An Intern stays an Intern until they sign up for Blazer People Network, at which point they become a Next Blazers Beneficiary (NBB).

## Program structure: cohort-based, not self-paced

NBC runs as **cohorts with fixed start dates**, not rolling individual enrolment. This is the highest-leverage design decision in the program, and the evidence behind it is unambiguous: self-paced online programs complete at roughly 12-15%, with about 70% of online courses going unfinished, while cohort-based programs land between 70% and 96%. Isolating the variables on a single platform's data: scheduled cohorts complete at 64.2% versus 48.2% for open-access self-paced, and adding community discussion moves completion from 42.6% to 65.5%.

Three structural requirements, each doing distinct work:

1. **Fixed-date cohorts of 8-12 participants.** Small enough for real peer accountability, with shared start and end dates that eliminate the procrastination self-pacing invites. Counterintuitive but well-supported: busy participants don't need more flexibility — flexibility is what produces indefinite postponement.
2. **A shared cohort discussion space.** The single cheapest intervention with the largest measured effect. Participants seeing peers move ahead is itself a motivator; roughly 71% of learners report their cohort motivates them to continue.
3. **Peer accountability partners within each cohort.** Pair participants to check on each other's progress — distinct from, and additional to, the sponsor relationship.

**Cohort structure is the primary mechanism; a modest fee re-enters as the specific consequence for missing a cohort's window entirely.** A fee makes a participant owe money for falling behind; a cohort makes them let down peers who notice — the evidence above says the second works considerably better as an everyday accountability mechanism, so it stays the default. In practice: each cohort runs in a dedicated group space (WhatsApp or Telegram, matching how this population already communicates day to day) rather than an app-only, individually-tracked experience. A participant falling behind their cohort's pace triggers a support check-in from their sponsor/NBX first — not a charge.

**If a participant still hasn't completed NBC by the end of their cohort's 60-day window, the choice is real and binary:** either their participant status lapses, or they pay **$25/month to join the next incoming cohort** instead. $25, not the original $33 — proportionate to NBC's own $90 total price, and realistic exposure is naturally bounded to roughly one cohort cycle's length rather than open-ended, since cohorts run on a regular cadence. **This grace option is capped at one use.** It buys entry into a single make-up cohort — if that one is also missed, participant status is lost outright, with no further fee-based extensions. Without the cap, an indefinitely payable fee stops functioning as a commitment signal and starts functioning as a permanent pay-to-delay option, which defeats its own purpose.

## Sponsor check-ins: protect what's already NBC's biggest advantage

The sponsor relationship (formalised in BPS Assignment 7) is a genuine structural asset most online programs lack entirely — programs that set structured goals in the first mentoring session report roughly 3x higher session completion rates. But its quality currently depends entirely on the individual sponsor, and the documented failure modes for exactly this kind of relationship are mentor overload and mismatched pairings, with structured mentor training identified as the critical protective factor.

**Give sponsors a written guide, not just an assignment prompt on the participant's side:** what a first check-in should cover (goal-setting specifically — that's where the 3x effect comes from), a standard set of check-in prompts, expected cadence, and what to escalate to admin. This is a low-cost addition that protects the program's strongest existing differentiator.

## Stage 1 — Onboarding (10 days)

Focus: understanding the gap the program exists to fill. One chapter of *Rich Dad Poor Dad* per day, ten days, ten chapters.

## Stage 2 — BPS Training (10 days)

NBO's own methodology for systemizing goal-setting and productivity. Nominally a 7-day course; reading *Goals* by Brian Tracy alongside it extends it to 10. Free YouTube videos are provided daily for context on each assignment.

**Seven assignments:**
1. Write 3 ways lack of direction has affected you; identify 3 people successful due to strong goal clarity (with interviews/quotes as evidence).
2. Convert 5 vague past goals into SMART format (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound); identify a tracking metric for each.
3. Take 3 of those SMART goals; for each, do 1-2 things within 48 hours to activate momentum (buy the domain, tell the mentor, draft the proposal); submit proof.
4. Personal SWOT analysis, plus a paragraph each on what to protect (strengths), overcome (weaknesses), pursue (opportunities), and avoid (threats), with action points.
5. Read and report on assigned *Goals* chapters in a notebook (photographed); build a tracking sheet — 4 chosen activities as columns, days of the week as rows, scored 1 or 0; submit the empty sheet, record weekly wins and lessons, comment privately after week one for admin review, rate consistency weekly.
6. The Monthly Goal Assignment — the core of BPS. See the dedicated section below.
7. Book a review/check-in with sponsor or team lead; prepare the Belief Goal, the tracking metrics, and the plan for Affirmation and Evaluation Goal submission; afterward submit the 3 main things discussed, any new commitments, and a screenshot (virtual) or summary (physical).

## Assignment 6 — The Monthly Goal System (the heart of BPS)

Framed explicitly as operational training, not motivational work: think 30 days ahead, set measurable goals, track effort rather than feelings, report truthfully and numerically, build discipline through binary execution.

**Prerequisites:** watch all provided videos with detailed handwritten notes (photographed as evidence); read *Goals* by Brian Tracy chapters 11-12 and write genuine understanding, not copied summaries.

**The 30-day planning rule (non-negotiable):** on the 1st of any month, you are already executing the *next* month's plan — January 1 means executing February's plan. This builds strategic distance and reduces emotional decision-making.

**Four pillars per month, each requiring 3 specific, observable, trackable action items:**
- **Finance goal** — income target and source; weeks 1-3 sales and marketing, week 4 skill training.
- **Self-development goal** — specific pages/chapters, daily or weekly reading targets, time commitment.
- **Multilevel marketing goal** — marked "NIL – Not yet a distributor" until the participant actually becomes one. No assumptions, no imagination.
- **Relationship goal with sponsor** — total sessions, split between virtual and physical, with stated purpose (review, mentorship, accountability).

### The Three Goals System

**This rhythm now extends well beyond NBC.** Daily binary tracking, weekly reporting, and this three-checkpoint structure govern DFY tracking, Rep oversight, and mentorship across all of DSE — for every participant, not just those who came through NBO. NBC is where it's first taught, not where its use ends. See `dfg`'s `references/bps-cadence.md` for the generalized version.

Three connected submissions per target month, at fixed times:

**1. Belief Goal** — submitted 30 days before the target month, to both sponsor and app admin. States intended *effort*, not results: a clear written goal, at least 3 measurable metrics, a task management board set up, and daily binary tracking begun (done = 1, not done = 0). Evidence: screenshots of the task board and metrics tracking.

**2. Affirmation Goal** — submitted 15 days before the target month. A 14-day effort report on the Belief Goal, per item: score (e.g. 8/14), percentage (57.14%), and a remark. **Remark language is strict — use verbatim, no creativity permitted:**
- 72% and above: "Effort is satisfactory. Goal set to be achieved"
- 60-71%: "Effort is minimal. Goal not taken seriously"
- Below 60%: "Effort is below requisite. Failure of goal is imminent"

**3. Evaluation Goal** — submitted on the 10th day of the target month. A 40-day effort evaluation from Belief Goal submission: score (e.g. 33/40), percentage (82.5%), and a remark. **Different thresholds and different wording from the Affirmation Goal — don't mix them up:**
- 75% and above: "My effort is satisfactory. Goal set will be achieved"
- 60-74%: "My effort has been minimal. This goal may not be achieved"
- Below 60%: "My effort has been poor. The goal is unmet"

Tracking may be done on paper or in Excel/Google Sheets and attached to the assignment or monthly report.

## Stage 3 — BEF Exposition Course (7 days)

Two halves, in sequence. First: what BEF is, its relationship to D'Famocious Group, and how it uses NBO to pursue its mission with students specifically. Then the course pivots entirely to NBO itself — its functionality, its three systems, and how a participant moves through them.

**Source content for the first half:** `bef`'s `references/identity-and-framework.md` and its `SKILL.md` — the B.L.A.Z.E.R. core values, the *Accende Potentiam* mantra, BEF's "intervene at the fragile transition from raw potential to productive capacity" framing, the Formal Covenant, the Pocket System, and the four-level discipline ladder with its fair-warning principle. This is also the natural place to state independent-contractor status and BEF's Catholic identity alongside its freedom-of-conscience commitment, since participants should hear both from the institution directly rather than infer them later.

Placed deliberately between BPS Training and Sales and Marketing: the participant has just built personal goal-setting discipline (stage 2) and is about to learn to sell (stage 4) — this stage is where they learn what organization they're actually inside and what it's for, before being asked to represent it.

## Stage 4 — Sales and Marketing (21 days)

Thorough sales and marketing instruction using the same daily-assignment format with supporting YouTube videos. Participant reads *$100M Offers* by Alex Hormozi daily alongside it. This is where the participant becomes an Intern.

## Stage 5 — DBI Introductory Course (10 days)

Introduces DBI itself, completing the 58-day core. Running concurrently, the participant begins a separate 40-day personal-development reading track — which means roughly 30 days of continued app use past the core program's end.

**Important:** once the DBI Introductory Course completes, the Intern is invited into Next Blazers Scholarship (the 90% DSE discount) and progression continues — the participant does *not* wait for the 30-day reading tail to finish first.

*Note on sequencing:* an earlier summary described Self Development as running concurrent with Sales and Marketing (stage 4). The detailed account above — self-development reading running concurrent with the DBI Introductory Course and extending 30 days past it — is the authoritative version. BEF's constitution separately describes a "60-Day Parallel Formation Policy" running alongside the *entire* NBC curriculum, from an earlier three-section architecture that the current five-stage system has since succeeded — that framing is superseded; use the version above.

**Confirmed superseded:** the Weekly Office Meeting (WOM, constitutionally led by NBX, supported by NBS) is discontinued as an NBO-run mechanism. Its function is now fulfilled through the DBI app rather than through NBO's own system — specifics on how not yet provided; don't invent them. Note this as distinct from the sponsor check-ins (BPS Assignment 7, the cohort structure above), which remain NBO's own mechanism and weren't what absorbed WOM's role.

## Non-circumvention and platform liability

**NBO's subsidized ($90) DSE access requires actually completing NBC — there's no shortcut into it.** This doesn't restrict DSE's other entry paths (regular $900, or SUC-direct entry), which never involved NBO in the first place; it specifically means someone can't claim NBO's subsidized rate without going through the program that subsidy is meant to fund.

**The platform can fail, and that's accounted for.** Participants acknowledge that technical disruption is possible; BEF isn't liable for progress lost to platform failure, and the Board of Stewards retains authority to institute an emergency alternative framework if the primary system goes down. Worth stating plainly rather than leaving it as an unstated risk.

## A good idea, premature at current scale

The constitution describes a monthly **Blazer Cheque Rally (BCR)** — celebrating that month's new BPN participants, distinct from the Annual Honours (`digital-systems-engineering`'s `references/annual-recognition.md`), which is yearly and reserved for standout achievement. The full execution model (NBA + BOV + NBS, tied to the Centre/Circle expansion hierarchy already flagged as premature) isn't appropriate yet. But the underlying idea — frequent, regular recognition, not just an annual ceremony — is a good one worth returning to in a simplified form once there's a real base of BPN participants to celebrate monthly.
