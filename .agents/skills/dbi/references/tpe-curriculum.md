# The Practice of Enterprise — Detailed Curriculum

*A proposed content architecture for the twelve TPE topics — what each week should cover, and how the twelve build into one continuous arc instead of twelve unrelated lessons. Offered as a working draft, in the same spirit as the alignment note in the original doc: worth adopting if it's useful, not asserted as settled.*

## The Throughline

The twelve topics group into six movements of two-to-three topics each. Each movement answers one underlying question; each topic inside it answers a piece of that question.

| Movement | Topics | Central Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | Order, Intelligence | Am I actually ready to build on — organized and thinking clearly? |
| 2. Direction & Connection | Leadership & Vision, Communication | Where are we going, and can I bring people with me? |
| 3. Capacity to Act | Time & Effort, Strategy & Innovation, Execution & Discipline | Can I move — and move on the right things? |
| 4. Scale & Durability | Systems-Building, Adaptability & Resilience | Does this survive without me, and survive a bad week? |
| 5. Resourcing | Financial Intelligence, Culture & Team Building | What fuels this — money and people — and am I handling both well? |
| 6. Integration | Sustainability & Ethics | Is this actually worth sustaining? |

Week 13 restarts at Topic 1 — not as a reset, but because Movement 6's question ("is this worth sustaining") is really asking whether the order from Movement 1 is still solid enough to keep building on. The loop isn't a limitation of the twelve-week format; it's the format mirroring how these particular skills actually work. None of them finish. They get maintained.

## Movement 1: Foundation

### 1. Order in Enterprise
*Order is the precondition, not a side virtue — the baseline that has to exist before strategy, leadership, or execution can work at all.*
- The difference between "busy" and "in order": order isn't doing more, it's knowing exactly where everything stands
- External order (one home for tasks, a clear workspace, a weekly reset) as the visible sign of internal clarity
- What disorder actually costs: missed commitments, decision fatigue, and the erosion of trust from people who depend on you
- A simple, portable practice to start the same day — a weekly reset: clear the inputs, confirm what's actually committed, name the week's top three
- Order as a leadership act: leaders who model it make it possible for the people around them to trust the structure

### 2. Intelligence in Enterprise
*Intelligence here means applied judgment — pattern recognition and decision quality — not credentials or raw IQ.*
- "Smart" versus "sharp": sharp is intelligence that's been tested against real decisions and real consequences
- Four practical intelligences worth building: analytical (numbers), emotional (people and situations), strategic (seeing several moves ahead), practical (getting things done inside real constraints)
- How intelligence actually gets built: deliberately, through what you read, who you talk to, and what you ask
- The two failure modes — overthinking into paralysis, or underthinking into impulsiveness — and learning which one you default to
- A decision-review habit: after anything significant, write down what you expected, what happened, and what you'd change

*Once someone is organized and thinking clearly, the next question is where all of that gets pointed — which is what vision and communication are for.*

## Movement 2: Direction & Connection

### 3. Leadership & Vision in Enterprise
*Vision is direction. Leadership is holding that direction clearly enough that other people can actually follow it.*
- Vision versus goals: vision answers "why does this matter," goals are the waypoints along the way
- Self-leadership first — you can't credibly lead others toward a standard you don't hold yourself to
- Leadership without a title: anyone can lead their piece of the enterprise, regardless of where they sit
- Trust as leadership's real currency — consistency between what's said and what's done, repeated long enough to be believed
- A vision-clarity exercise: describe, specifically enough to notice drift, what "working" looks like a year out

### 4. Communication in Enterprise
*Most breakdowns in enterprise are communication breakdowns, not competence breakdowns. Clarity is a discipline, not a talent some people are born with.*
- Clarity over cleverness: the goal is to be understood, not to sound impressive
- Listening as the neglected half of communication — most people transmit; few actually receive
- Adjusting the message to the audience: a mentor, a peer, and a prospect all need something different from the same information
- Handling a hard conversation without damaging the relationship — saying the true thing while staying kind
- Choosing the channel on purpose: knowing when a written report is enough and when it actually needs a real conversation

*Direction and the ability to communicate it are still just potential energy until they turn into actual movement — which is what Topics 5 through 7 are for.*

## Movement 3: Capacity to Act

### 5. Time & Effort in Enterprise
*Time and effort are both finite. The goal was never to spend more of them — it's to spend them on the right things.*
- Why effort and output aren't the same thing — the trap of mistaking busyness for progress
- A simple weekly filter: what's the one thing this week that actually moves the needle
- Protecting real focus time for what matters, instead of only reacting to whatever's loudest
- Energy management alongside time management — matching demanding work to when you're actually sharp
- Why consistency beats intensity: steady effort sustained for months outperforms sporadic bursts

### 6. Strategy & Innovation in Enterprise
*Strategy is the discipline of deliberate choice. What it excludes matters as much as what it includes.*
- Strategy as focus — trying to win everywhere usually means winning nowhere
- Where innovation actually comes from: constraints and unmet needs, not sudden inspiration
- Balancing doing the proven thing better against trying something genuinely new
- Staying aware of what's shifting outside, not just managing what's already inside
- Innovating through small, cheap experiments instead of single big bets

### 7. Execution & Discipline in Enterprise
*The distance between a good idea and a good result is made entirely of execution. Discipline is what closes it.*
- Why execution, not ideas, is the scarce resource — everyone has ideas, few follow all the way through
- Building systems for follow-through instead of depending on motivation, which is unreliable by design
- Accountability as support, not surveillance — a structure that helps you keep commitments, not one that just watches for failure
- The compounding cost of things left unfinished, against the compounding trust built by finishing what's started
- Discipline as self-respect: keeping commitments to yourself is what makes it possible to keep them to anyone else

*Movement without a system behind it doesn't scale, and it doesn't survive a bad week. Topics 8 and 9 are what make progress repeatable and durable instead of a one-time push.*

## Movement 4: Scale & Durability

### 8. Systems-Building in Enterprise
*The shift from individual effort to repeatable systems is what lets an enterprise grow without growing more chaotic.*
- The difference between doing a task and building a system for that task
- Documenting what works well enough that it can be repeated, and taught to someone else
- Spotting the repetitive decisions quietly costing time that are actually worth systematizing
- Why systems are what let something scale — Order (Topic 1) applied to the whole enterprise, not just one person
- Keeping systems in service of people rather than the reverse — a system nobody can adapt has already become a liability

### 9. Adaptability & Resilience in Enterprise
*Things will go wrong regardless of how well anything is planned. What matters is the capacity to absorb it and keep moving.*
- Resilience isn't just toughness — it includes honestly reckoning with what went wrong and asking for support when it's needed
- Adaptability as a trainable skill: practicing flexible thinking before circumstance forces it
- Separating signal from noise in a setback — what genuinely needs to change versus what's just discomfort
- Why resilience is rarely a solo act — the people around you are part of how you recover, not proof you couldn't do it alone
- The case that some setbacks, faced honestly, leave things stronger than before, not just repaired

*None of this runs on willpower alone. It runs on two kinds of fuel — money and people — which is what Topics 10 and 11 are about.*

## Movement 5: Resourcing

### 10. Financial Intelligence in Enterprise
*Fluency with money — understanding it, protecting it, growing it — is a skill like any other, and it has to be taught on purpose.*
- Knowing your own numbers: what's actually coming in, what's going out, and what's genuinely left over
- Income versus wealth — cash flow keeps things running, assets are what's actually being built
- Practical discipline: keeping a reserve, resisting lifestyle creep, reinvesting on purpose rather than by accident
- Reading the basic financial health of your part of the enterprise, whatever that looks like in your role
- A patient view of money: real financial intelligence includes healthy skepticism toward anything promising fast, effortless returns

### 11. Culture & Team Building in Enterprise
*Enterprise is never really solo. The people and culture around someone shape outcomes as much as any individual skill does.*
- Culture as "what's normal here" — the unwritten expectations that shape behavior more than any official rule
- Building enough trust and psychological safety that problems surface early instead of getting hidden
- What gets recognized gets repeated — the role of specific, genuine recognition in reinforcing good behavior
- Building around complementary strengths instead of expecting every contributor to be the same shape
- Culture as something shaped on purpose — left alone, it still forms, just by accident

*Which leaves the question underneath all eleven other topics: is any of this actually worth sustaining? Topic 12 is the capstone — and the hinge back to Topic 1.*

## Movement 6: Integration

### 12. Sustainability & Ethics in Enterprise
*An enterprise that can't sustain itself — financially, ethically, personally — hasn't actually succeeded, whatever it looks like in the short term.*
- Long-term thinking over short-term extraction: practices that don't quietly burn out people, resources, or trust
- Ethics as the foundation of a reputation that lasts — trust is slow to build and fast to lose
- Personal sustainability: keeping enterprise integrated with life rather than sacrificing one for the other
- A simple test for gray areas: who's affected by this, and would it hold up if everyone involved could see it
- The closing loop: sustaining any of this depends on the same order that started the cycle — which is exactly why Week 13 goes back to Topic 1 instead of moving on to something new

## Designing for the Revisit

The premise behind the quarterly loop is that the same video lands differently in month 2 versus month 14. That's true — but only halfway for free. A participant's life changes on its own. The video doesn't, unless something is built to make it change with them. Two low-cost ways to make the revisit deliberate instead of just hoped-for:

- **Separate the principle from the prompt.** Keep the core video content stable — it doesn't need reshooting every quarter — but pair each week with a single reflection question that rotates each time the topic comes back around. The video stays evergreen; the prompt is what makes cycle four feel different from cycle one.
- **Let the BPS Cadence report double as the reflection.** Since Monday already carries both the TPE video and the weekly BPS report, one line in that report could ask a participant to connect the week's topic to something real from their own week. That turns "watched" into "used," without standing up a second system.

## A Few Open Questions Worth Deciding Early

- Does the video content itself ever get refreshed, or does "the revisit lands differently" rely entirely on the participant changing and never on the content changing?
- Who actually holds the support conversation when someone accumulates missed weeks — is there a light script or set of prompts for the Rep or mentor, so "support, not punishment" is something participants experience, not just a line in a policy doc?
- Is there any lightweight way to know whether a video actually landed — a reaction, a one-line takeaway — beyond the binary "interacted with it," or is exposure itself the whole point of TPE, with practice expected to happen elsewhere in the ecosystem?
