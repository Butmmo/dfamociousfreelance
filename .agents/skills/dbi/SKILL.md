# D'Famocious Business Incubator (DBI)

A subsidiary of D'Famocious Group (DFG) — for the parent company's own identity and governance, load `dfg`. DBI trains and incubates operators through two sequential arms — Digital Systems Engineering (DSE, Stage 1) and Start-up Catalyst (SUC, Stage 2) — and takes a real equity/revenue stake in what it helps build, rather than operating as a pure training program. This skill is the router and the home for what's true across both arms; it does not duplicate either arm's own depth, and it does not duplicate DFG's governance content either.

**Working reference: DBI Constitution (Restated).** This skill implements and elaborates that document operationally; where the two differ, the Constitution governs.

## The two arms, and when to load which skill

- **Digital Systems Engineering (DSE)** — Stage 1. Seven service-based, client-acquisition systems (Ascent, CareBridge, Ministry Systems, Revenue Recovery, SMB Optimization, Broadcast Engine, Authority Engine) a student runs to build cash flow and proof. Niche selection, prospecting, closing, pricing tiers, DFY, certification → load `digital-systems-engineering`.
- **Start-up Catalyst (SUC)** — Stage 2. Fourteen paths (seven Compounder digital products, seven Proprietor traditional businesses) for turning DSE-proven earning ability into an owned business. Design, structure, testing, launch, valuation, premium/luxury positioning → load `startup-catalyst`.
- **This skill** — anything spanning both: the full pricing journey, DBI's own structure, DBI-BEF relationship, or routing an ambiguous question to the right arm.

## The full journey, pricing end to end

DSE entry: $900 (or $90 via BEF's NBO subsidy). Once earning, DFY applies — 20% of every earning month back to DBI, for one qualified year (12 qualified months of at least $2,000 net income each, not necessarily consecutive; sub-threshold months still owe the 20%, they just don't advance the count — deliberate, to build urgency and consistency). Completing DFY earns the Vetted Digital Systems Engineer (V. DsE.) certification.

DSE produces SUC eligibility once it generates $450,000 in cumulative earnings within 3-5 years (or an equivalent amount over a longer timeframe, per the tiered calculator — $90,000/year continuously from year 5 onward) — outcome-based, not simply time elapsed — plus the Leadership by Influence standard (5 verified mentee DFY completions, or verified retail sales for BPN-active candidates). If invited, SUC entry is $2,000 (DBI's standard 25% equity). Skipping DSE entirely costs $6,000 (same 25% equity) or $24,000 (DBI's equity reduced to 10% instead) — a deliberate ladder, not a cliff, so DBI stays genuinely aligned with the business at every tier rather than holding no stake at all in the top tier.

A formula for individually timing SUC invitations and subsidizing SUC tuition — based on each beneficiary's actual earning curve rather than the flat thresholds above — is still under study. Treat the numbers above as current policy, not as the final word on how personalized this could eventually become.

Full calibration reasoning for the pricing ladder, and the Leadership by Influence standard in full: `digital-systems-engineering`'s `references/pricing-and-dfy.md` and `references/leadership-by-influence.md`. Full ownership mechanics and exceptions (License, Franchise/Acquisition): `startup-catalyst`'s Ownership section.

## DBI and BEF

BEF is DFG's NGO sister subsidiary, not DBI's own — see `dfg` for how the two relate at the parent level. The direct DBI-BEF financial relationship: DBI reimburses BEF a flat **$1,000 per NBC intern who completes DFY** — recognition of NBC's role in preparing that beneficiary, tied to full DFY completion specifically (not mere sign-up), so the payment rewards genuine successful development, not volume. A percentage-of-net-income claim was considered and deliberately rejected: disproportionate to BEF's actual contribution, manipulable through DBI's own accounting choices, and it would have given BEF a financial stake in DBI's overall profitability that could compromise BEF's independence in exactly the moments that independence matters most. This reimbursement is a related-party transaction and should go through BEF's own Conflict of Interest review process (`bef`'s `references/identity-and-framework.md`), not operate as an informal understanding.

SUC eligibility no longer depends on any Neolife rank — it runs through DBI's own Leadership by Influence standard instead → load `digital-systems-engineering`.

## The Practice of Enterprise (TPE)

WOM's real successor, not just a note that it moved into the app. A 12-topic curriculum in six thematic movements (Order, Intelligence, Leadership & Vision, Communication, Time & Effort, Strategy & Innovation, Execution & Discipline, Systems-Building, Adaptability & Resilience, Financial Intelligence, Culture & Team Building, Sustainability & Ethics — all "in Enterprise"), one pre-recorded video per week, fixed to Monday, revolving continuously across roughly quarterly cycles — for every DSE and SUC participant, not NBO-specific. Binary completion tracking plus an opt-in signal layer (a single-tap reaction, an optional line in the weekly BPS report) for whether it's actually landing, not just being watched. Full detail across all three: `references/tpe.md`, `references/tpe-curriculum.md`, `references/tpe-signal-layer.md`.

**One thing worth flagging rather than quietly resolving:** earlier tracking in this ecosystem referred to "BEF's still-unnamed business-funding initiative for SUC-stage businesses," with The Panel (`bef`) as its governance precursor. BSA's "startup capital where possible" may be that initiative now named, or it may be a distinct thing — BSA's capital assistance reads as aimed at its own trainees becoming employable or self-employed, which isn't obviously the same population as already-formed, DBI-equity-bearing SUC businesses. Don't assume these are the same until confirmed.

## Certification

Vetted Digital Systems Engineer (V. DsE.) is earned on DFY completion at the DSE stage — a newly created credential, revocable for post-graduation fraud or ethics breach, not a pre-existing industry standard. No SUC-stage completion certification has been defined yet; if asked, say so rather than inventing one.
