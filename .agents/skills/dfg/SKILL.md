# D'Famocious Group (DFG)

The parent holding company. DFG is not itself a training program or an incubator — it holds two subsidiaries, D'Famocious Business Incubator (DBI, for-profit, `dbi`) and Blazer Empowerment Foundation (BEF, NGO, `bef`), and its own constitution is the authoritative source for governance structure both subsidiaries reference rather than each maintaining their own version. This skill exists specifically because that content had been drifting into subsidiary-level files as paraphrase — it's the single source now.

**Working reference: DFG Constitution (Restated).** This skill implements and elaborates that document operationally; where the two differ, the Constitution governs.

## DFG is a talent pipeline, not a training vendor — this decides how every relationship in the ecosystem should read

DFG develops operators and captures a return on that development; it doesn't simply sell an education product. This is *why* the financial instrument changes across stages, not an inconsistency to explain away: DSE uses DFY (a revenue-share) because at Stage 1 there's no business yet to hold equity in — the beneficiary is building earning capacity working other people's offers, not owning an asset yet. Once a real business exists (SUC), the instrument switches to actual equity, because now there's something to hold equity *in*.

**What this changes in practice:** every DSE-stage relationship — the Rep, the Auditor, DFY, discipline — should read as an investor's ongoing interest in a developing asset, not an employer's authority over a worker. An investor monitors, supports, and verifies. An employer disciplines.

## Governance: four distinct authorities, not one Founder with unlimited reach

- **The Founder** — perpetual, doctrinal status as originator. Not an executive office.
- **The Chancellor** — Chief Doctrinal Officer and Custodian of the Institutional Seal. **Explicitly non-executive**: "holds no direct executive authority over the Group's operational or fiscal management... exclusively custodial and advisory." A non-voting, ex-officio member and secretariat of the Elder Council, not a parallel decision-maker alongside it. One narrow exception: in a genuine emergency where a material doctrinal breach is imminent and the Elder Council can't convene, the Chancellor alone may issue a temporary doctrinal stay — a real but tightly-scoped emergency power, not standing authority.
- **The DFG Elder Council** (5-12 members, five-year renewable terms, up to three consecutive) holds supreme doctrinal veto over anything conflicting with DFG's Core Principles or Catholic Identity, exercised through a formal Doctrinal Nullity Declaration requiring a two-thirds vote — and is the final, binding authority for constitutional-interpretation and discipline/suspension appeals that reach that level. It has no legislative, executive, or day-to-day operational authority.
- **The DFG Board of Stewards and CEO** hold supreme authority over operational, fiscal, and long-term strategic functions — day-to-day executive oversight, budget compliance, and strategic implementation, with formal reporting back to the Board. This is the body meant to provide ongoing operational governance.

**"Seminal stage, Founder still hands-on" describes the Board of Stewards/CEO layer not yet being fully staffed and independent — it is not the Chancellor's authority expanding to fill that gap.** The fix for the gap is a real Board of Stewards, not a broader Chancellor role.

**No unilateral financial control, at any level.** No single individual may exercise unilateral financial control outside documented limits set by the Board — this applies to the Founder as much as anyone else.

**Conflict of interest, DFG level:** any Steward, Officer, or Legislative Committee member must declare an actual or perceived conflict before deliberating or voting on a motion; failure to declare is itself a conduct violation, and recused members don't count toward quorum for that vote. This is a narrower, voting-procedure rule — distinct from BEF's own broader, NGO-focused conflict-of-interest policy (related-party transactions, gifts, self-dealing sanctions), which governs BEF's operations specifically and isn't replaced by this. See `bef`'s `references/identity-and-framework.md` for that fuller version.

**One deliberate, scoped exception to the Elder Council's normal composition:** the D'Famocious Innovator of the Year (the SUC founder with the year's highest audited profit) receives a special one-year Elder Council seat — full presence and voice, but excluding the doctrinal-veto vote itself, keeping that specific power reserved to Elders vetted for doctrinal judgment rather than business performance. Full detail: `digital-systems-engineering`'s `references/annual-recognition.md`.

## Why DBI and BEF are both necessary, in the clearest version of this on record

DSE (DBI's Stage 1) proves earning capacity. BEF proves leadership and institutional readiness. Together they're the pipeline into enterprise formation — neither substitutes for the other, and a student strong in one isn't presumed strong in both.

## The BPS Cadence — the actual mechanism behind human capital transformation, not just an NBC assignment

Daily binary self-tracking, a weekly reporting cadence, and a three-checkpoint 40-day goal cycle (Belief → Affirmation → Evaluation) — first taught inside NBC's Blazer Productivity Scheme, but the rhythm itself, not the coursework, is the mechanism. It governs reporting, mentorship, sponsorship, and follow-up everywhere in the ecosystem, for every DSE and SUC participant, whether they passed through NBO or not. Full detail and how it applies to DFY tracking, Rep oversight, and mentorship specifically: `references/bps-cadence.md`.

**Founder-operators are custodians, not absolute owners, of any DFG-supported enterprise** — this applies to SUC businesses under DBI (see `startup-catalyst`'s "no rogue exits" provision) and to any enterprise BEF's initiatives eventually spin out. Institutional support may be withdrawn on ethical breach, governance refusal, or persistent mission deviation — real, defined grounds, not open-ended discretion.

## A terminology collision worth flagging clearly, not silently avoiding

DFS's original constitution used "DBI" as shorthand for "D'Famocious Beneficiary Income" — the beneficiary's 60% earnings share under the now-superseded split. That has nothing to do with D'Famocious Business Incubator, the current subsidiary — same three letters, coincidental, from an unrelated document. Never use "DBI" to mean beneficiary income in anything written now; if the historical document comes up, name the collision rather than letting it pass unremarked.

## Honest marketing and outcome disclosure

Governs recruitment and promotional material across DBI, BEF, and every initiative — separate from DFY's own participant-facing disclosure, though the two should never contradict each other. Prohibited claims (guaranteed income, unrepresentative success stories, "risk-free" framing, overstated completion/placement stats), required disclosures (realistic outcome ranges, plain-language DFY awareness, clear optional-vs-required labeling), and a lightweight second-person review before anything publishes — grounded directly in the specific failures (BloomTech, Prehired) that made this necessary elsewhere. Full detail: `references/honest-marketing.md`.
