# Honest Marketing & Outcome Disclosure

Governs DBI/BEF/NBO's own recruitment and promotional material — website copy, social media, testimonial features, cohort recruitment posts. **Distinct from DFY's own disclosure requirements** (`digital-systems-engineering`'s `references/pricing-and-dfy.md`, which governs what a participant is told once they're actually entering DFY) — this file covers what gets said *before* someone signs up, and DFY should still be referenced honestly here too, not siloed off to a later contractual moment.

Also distinct from how individual DSE graduates market their own client-facing service businesses (Ascent's scripts, CareBridge's outreach, etc.) — that's covered by the free-work correction already built into each DSE system's file. This is about DBI's own institutional voice, not a graduate's.

## Why this exists, grounded in what actually happened elsewhere

Every rule below maps to a specific, documented failure, not a hypothetical one. BloomTech advertised 71-86% job placement while its own internal investor reporting showed placement closer to 50%, sometimes 30%. Prehired guaranteed a "6-figure sales career" and marketed "zero upfront costs" while burying terms that required payment even without a job. Both drew multi-million-dollar federal enforcement and permanent bans. Separately, MLM-adjacent recruitment of young people has its own documented pattern (Vemma's "Young People Revolution" campaign, built on exactly the kind of income-potential framing this file exists to prevent) — directly relevant given BPN sits inside this ecosystem.

## Prohibited in any recruitment or promotional material

- **No guaranteed income claims** — "you will earn $X" in any form, implied or explicit.
- **No unrepresentative success stories presented as typical**, without a realistic range stated alongside. A real testimonial is fine; a real testimonial standing in for "what you should expect" is the BloomTech pattern.
- **No "risk-free," "no strings attached," or "not a loan/not debt" framing** — anywhere, not just in formal DFY disclosure. If DFY is mentioned or implied, it gets described honestly or not at all.
- **No overstated completion, placement, or earnings statistics.** Any number used must be real, current, and not cherry-picked from a favorable cohort — if the honest number is unflattering, don't publish a flattering one instead; don't publish the number.
- **No implying BPN participation is required, or that it reliably produces income.** MLM participation is optional everywhere in this ecosystem, and the honest base rate — most MLM participants do not profit — should not be contradicted by recruitment tone even when it isn't explicitly denied.

## Required in recruitment or promotional material

- **A realistic outcome range**, not just best-case examples, wherever outcomes are discussed at all.
- **Plain-language DFY awareness** wherever DSE is being promoted — not the full disclosure statement necessarily, but enough that "there's a revenue-share obligation once you're earning" isn't a surprise discovered later.
- **Clear labeling of what's optional versus required** — BPN, active selling within BPN, and any other optional element stated as optional, not implied as expected.
- **Realistic time-to-outcome framing** — no language implying fast or guaranteed results.

## Review before publication

Whoever writes promotional material is not the one who clears it — the same separation-of-verification principle running through DFY (Rep/Auditor) and Leadership by Influence (mentor self-reports, Rep certifies) applies here too. A second person checks material against both lists above before it goes out. At current scale this doesn't need a formal committee — one other person's sign-off, logged somewhere real, is enough to make the check actually happen rather than being skipped under deadline pressure. Formalize further only if volume grows enough to need it.
