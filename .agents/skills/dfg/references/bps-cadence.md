# The BPS Cadence

The Blazer Productivity Scheme (`nbo`'s `references/nbc-curriculum.md`) teaches a specific rhythm inside NBC's 40-day window — but the rhythm itself is the actual mechanism, not the NBC coursework it happens to be taught through. This file extracts that rhythm as a standalone methodology, meant to govern reporting, mentorship, sponsorship, and follow-up everywhere in the ecosystem — **for every DSE and SUC participant, whether they passed through NBO or not.** Someone who paid full price and never touched NBC still gets tracked, mentored, and followed up on this same rhythm; they just never did the NBC coursework that originally taught it.

## Why this, specifically, is the core mechanism

Three nested layers, each doing different work:

1. **Daily binary self-tracking.** A small number of chosen activities, scored 1 (done) or 0 (not done) each day. No partial credit, no self-narrative — a day either happened or it didn't. This is what makes effort measurable rather than felt.
2. **A weekly reporting cadence.** Regular, predictable submission of that daily tracking to whoever is providing oversight — a sponsor, a Rep, a mentor. Weekly is frequent enough to catch drift early, infrequent enough to be sustainable.
3. **A three-checkpoint, ~40-day goal cycle.** A Belief Goal (commitment to effort, set roughly 30 days ahead, with real measurable metrics — intended effort, not a promised result), an Affirmation Goal (a mid-cycle check, roughly 15 days in, with a percentage score and fixed, non-improvised remark language reflecting the effort level), and an Evaluation Goal (an end-of-cycle result, roughly 40 days from Belief submission, with its own percentage score and its own fixed remark language — genuinely different wording from the Affirmation Goal's, not interchangeable).

The daily layer generates the data. The weekly layer keeps oversight current rather than retrospective. The 40-day cycle is what turns raw activity into a real, structured pursuit of a specific goal, with a beginning, a checkpoint, and an end — rather than open-ended effort with no shape to it.

## Applying it beyond NBC

- **DFY tracking (DSE):** each qualifying period runs its own Belief → Affirmation → Evaluation cycle, not just a pass/fail check against the $2,000 monthly threshold at the end. A beneficiary commits to a specific income-generating plan roughly 30 days out, gets a real mid-cycle check on effort, and gets evaluated at the end — the same structure that already governs NBC's goals now governs the pursuit of each qualified month itself. Full application: `digital-systems-engineering`'s `references/pricing-and-dfy.md`.
- **Rep oversight (DSE):** a Rep's "guidance, metric collection, performance tracking" (`references/rep-and-auditor.md`) is this cadence in practice — daily data reviewed on a weekly cadence, with the 40-day cycle providing the actual goal structure a Rep is tracking someone against, not a vague ongoing watchfulness.
- **Leadership by Influence mentorship (DSE):** mentor-mentee check-ins move to this cadence rather than running on an arbitrary schedule — see `references/leadership-by-influence.md` for how quarterly check-ins were replaced.
- **SUC:** no existing tracking mechanism currently retrofits this the way DSE's does, since SUC doesn't yet have a Rep-equivalent oversight role — but any future SUC-stage progress or mentorship system should adopt this same cadence for consistency across the whole DBI pipeline, rather than inventing a different rhythm. Flagged as the intended standard, not yet built out in `startup-catalyst`.

## What doesn't change

The exact remark language for Affirmation and Evaluation Goals stays specific to NBC's own BPS assignments (`nbo`'s `references/nbc-curriculum.md`) — don't invent parallel wording for DFY or mentorship applications unless it's actually decided. What generalizes is the *rhythm* (daily, weekly, 40-day-three-checkpoint) — not necessarily every piece of NBC-specific phrasing wrapped around it.
