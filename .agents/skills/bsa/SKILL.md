# Blazing Skill Acquisition (BSA)

BEF's second initiative (`bef`'s `SKILL.md` names all three: NBO built, BSA in development, BLI undeveloped). As of this writing, genuinely nothing is built yet — this file captures foundational strategy, not an operating program. More hands-on than NBO: teaching skills and upgrading professionals, rather than subsidizing DSE entry.

**Working reference: BSA Constitution (Restated)** — itself honestly incomplete, with Part VII naming what isn't yet decided rather than inventing it. This skill implements and elaborates what the Constitution does state; where both are silent, neither should be assumed to fill the gap.

## Mandate, as given

Elevate skilled people and professionals into modern relevance, place them into gainful employment — with a specific referral channel into SUC-incubated startups — and assist with startup capital where possible. Aims to become a leading employee-development engine in Africa, starting with Nigeria.

**One thing flagged rather than resolved:** whether BSA's "startup capital where possible" is the same as the previously-tracked "BEF SUC-funding initiative" placeholder, or a distinct thing — BSA's capital assistance reads as aimed at its own trainees, not necessarily the same population as already-formed SUC businesses. Don't assume these are the same until confirmed (`dbi`'s `SKILL.md` carries the same flag).

## Pricing: general, not per-skill — one price across all trades, not seven

A small, non-zero entry fee produces better completion and commitment than a fully free program — the same principle already validated for NBC's $90 fee, not re-derived from scratch. **The pricing structure itself is uniform across BSA, not differentiated per trade:** one base price, one discount rate, regardless of which of the seven skills below someone chooses. Differentiated per-skill pricing would make trade choice a financial decision rather than an aptitude one, and would sit awkwardly against BEF's access-oriented mission — the same instinct that's kept other parts of this ecosystem (The Panel, DFY's disclosure) deliberately unranked and non-comparative. Duration can still vary honestly by trade even with a uniform price; forcing every trade into the same timebox just to preserve price uniformity would be the wrong trade-off. Treat this as the launch position — revisit once there's real per-trade delivery-cost data, not before. The specific figure isn't set; it needs BSA's own real cost structure to discount against, the same way NBC's $90/$900 split was grounded in an actual subsidy gap.

## Skills focus: two distinct offer lists, not one blended set

Full research and reasoning: `references/skills-strategy.md`. Two different questions, kept deliberately separate:

- **Ten self-employable trades** — what a BSA graduate can run independently: electrical, plumbing, welding, solar/renewable energy, automotive/EV maintenance, tailoring, hairdressing/cosmetology, tiling, painting, carpentry. Nigeria has a severe, well-documented artisan shortage acute enough that skilled trade work is routinely filled by foreign labor — nothing else in this ecosystem touches this territory.
- **Ten SUC-employability professional offers** — what SUC businesses, across all fourteen paths, would actually need to hire someone for: customer support, virtual assistant/ops, bookkeeping, content support, retail sales, hospitality service, logistics coordination, facilities/light maintenance, technical/product support, community coordination. Every offer pairs a soft skill with a hard or physical one, mapped to specific SUC paths rather than generic — and genuinely portable to any employer, not SUC-exclusive.
- **Hard/technical skills — deliberately distinct from DSE's territory.** DSE already owns digital client-acquisition systems. BSA's lane: foundational digital literacy and specific named employer gaps (version control, communicating data insights clearly, cybersecurity fundamentals).
- **Soft skills — reuse TPE rather than build a second curriculum.** TPE already covers this territory (Communication, Leadership & Vision, Culture & Team Building) — route BSA trainees through it rather than duplicating (`dbi`'s `references/tpe.md`).

## Funding: a real sequence, not one ask

Full detail and sourcing: `references/funding-strategy.md`. In brief — Tier 1 (now, no track record needed): small rolling-basis grants explicitly open to new organizations. Tier 2 (once a real pilot exists): larger early-stage-social-enterprise funders. Tier 3 (later, with real outcomes data): major foundation partnerships, typically reached through existing partners rather than cold applications. **Internal bootstrap funding from DBI/BEF should come first, before any external tier** — funders above Tier 1 consistently want to see something operating before they'll back it.
