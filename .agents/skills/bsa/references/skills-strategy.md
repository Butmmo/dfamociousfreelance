# BSA Skills Strategy: Hard, Soft, Physical

Grounded in current (2026) Nigerian labor-market research, not assumption. Three categories, each with a distinct rationale for why BSA should own it.

## Physical/trade skills — the clearest, most distinctive lane

Nigeria has a severe, well-documented artisan shortage. Skilled trade work — plumbing, tiling, electrical installation, welding, masonry — is routinely filled by Beninoise and Togolese craftsmen rather than trained Nigerians, and the gap is described directly as a national economic constraint, not just a construction-industry headache. The opportunity isn't generic old-style apprenticeship training; multiple sources specifically flag *modernized* trade skills as the differentiator — green building techniques, electric vehicle maintenance, industrial plumbing, solar installation, automation systems layered onto traditional trades.

**Why this belongs to BSA specifically:** nothing else in the DBI/BEF ecosystem touches physical trades at all. DSE is digital-systems work; NBO/NBC is pre-DSE preparation. This is genuinely open territory, not a duplicate of anything already built.

### Ten trades to launch with

Selected specifically for where soft-skills training changes the outcome, not just for demand alone — trades where a practitioner's communication and professionalism are currently the real limiting factor, not their technical competence. The research pattern was consistent: technically able artisans losing work to unreliability and poor communication, not lack of skill.

1. **Electrical installation & maintenance** — universal demand; reliability and trust are named directly as what separates a booked-solid artisan from one who isn't.
2. **Plumbing** — same trust-dependency, same client-facing demand.
3. **Welding** — more B2B (construction, manufacturing) than the others, but reliability and clear site communication remain real, valuable soft-skill applications.
4. **Solar/renewable energy installation** — the modernized, higher-ticket trade specifically flagged as underserved. A bigger financial decision for the client means explaining the system and managing expectations honestly matters more here than almost anywhere else on this list.
5. **Automotive/EV maintenance** — a trade where distrust is practically cultural in Nigeria; transparent communication and honest pricing could be a bigger competitive edge here than in any other trade on this list.
6. **Tailoring** — strong existing self-employment culture, genuinely client-facing (fittings, style consultation), with a natural growth path from skilled tailor to small fashion business for someone who also has the professional polish to build repeat clientele.
7. **Hairdressing/cosmetology/barbing** — sustained personal client interaction; the research itself noted successful practitioners already use social media to build their businesses, meaning this trade already rewards exactly the communication and marketing skills BSA would be adding.
8. **Tiling** — same artisan-shortage pattern as electrical and plumbing; homeowner/contractor-facing, trust and finish-quality dependent.
9. **Painting** — same pattern again; direct client interaction, and reliability (showing up, finishing on schedule) is repeatedly named as the actual differentiator in this trade specifically.
10. **Carpentry** — chosen deliberately alongside tiling and painting, not just to round out the number: these three work together constantly on the same real projects, giving BSA graduates in these three trades a natural basis to cross-refer work to each other.

Shape of the list, worth keeping deliberate rather than incidental: three core infrastructure trades, one modernized/premium trade, one trust-sensitive trade, two personal-service trades with strong self-employment paths, and three finishing trades that naturally cluster together on real jobs — not ten variations on the same thing.

### Ten professional offers for employability inside SUC businesses — a different question from self-employment

The ten trades above answer "what can someone run independently." This answers a different one: what SUC businesses, across all fourteen paths, would actually need to hire someone for — mapped against SUC's real structure, not generic guessing. Every offer pairs a soft skill with either a hard or a physical skill; none stands on soft skills alone.

1. **Customer Support & Client Success** (empathetic communication + CRM/ticketing proficiency) — near-universal across Compounder paths, especially Micro-SaaS and Membership Community.
2. **Virtual Assistant & Operations Support** (reliability/organization + scheduling and admin tools) — the role almost every solo SUC founder needs first, digital or traditional.
3. **Bookkeeping & Financial Administration** (trustworthiness/attention to detail + bookkeeping software) — especially the cash-heavy Proprietor paths: Retail, Food/Hospitality, Agro-Processing.
4. **Content & Social Media Support** (brand-voice consistency + content/scheduling tools) — Membership Community, Course, Retail, Food/Hospitality.
5. **Retail Sales & Merchandising** (persuasion/customer service + merchandising, POS, inventory display) — Retail & Consumer Trading specifically.
6. **Hospitality & Guest Service** (warmth/conflict resolution + food and event service) — Food, Beverage & Hospitality specifically.
7. **Logistics & Inventory Coordination** (vendor communication/problem-solving + warehouse and inventory management) — Import/Export, Retail, Agro-Processing.
8. **Facilities & Light Maintenance Support** (responsiveness/professionalism + basic repair and upkeep) — Real Estate, Franchise/Acquisition, Local Service. Deliberately bridges the two lists — a direct use for BSA's own electrical and plumbing trade training inside SUC employment, not a separately invented skill.
9. **Technical/Product Support** (patience and clear non-technical explanation + troubleshooting and QA) — Micro-SaaS Tool and any Compounder digital product with real users.
10. **Community & Student Success Coordination** (mentorship/facilitation + community-platform management) — Membership Community, Course, Mastermind/Certification.

Shape worth naming here too: six lean toward hard/digital skills (Compounder-side demand), four toward physical skills (Proprietor-side demand) — roughly mirroring SUC's own even split between the two tracks, not incidental. These are also genuinely portable — every one of the ten is a real function at companies with no connection to DBI at all, so a BSA graduate isn't limited to SUC-incubated employers specifically, just especially well-positioned for them.

## Hard/technical skills — deliberately not DSE's territory

DSE already owns digital client-acquisition systems (Ascent, CareBridge, Ministry Systems, Revenue Recovery, SMB Optimization, Broadcast Engine, Authority Engine) — BSA shouldn't rebuild that. Instead, foundational digital literacy and the specific gaps Nigerian employers name directly:

- **Cybersecurity and networking fundamentals** — 87% of Nigerian employers identify these as critical hiring criteria, well above the 70% global average.
- **Practical tool fluency, not just conceptual knowledge** — employers specifically flag candidates who can run analysis but can't explain results to non-technical audiences, and applicants with no real project portfolio (e.g., no GitHub history for tech-adjacent roles).
- **Data literacy** — working with data in standard tools, interpreting basic analysis, presenting findings clearly. Cuts across sectors, cited as a consistent gap, and learnable to a competitive level in a few months of focused practice.

## Soft skills — reuse TPE, don't rebuild it

Communication, teamwork, adaptability, and professionalism are the most consistently cited employer gaps across every source checked, including specific complaints about logical thinking, negotiation, and administrative follow-through. TPE (`dbi`'s `references/tpe.md`, `references/tpe-curriculum.md`) already covers exactly this territory across its twelve topics — Communication in Enterprise, Leadership & Vision, Culture & Team Building, and others map directly onto the named gaps. Route BSA trainees through TPE for this component rather than building a parallel soft-skills curriculum from scratch — the same reuse-over-duplication principle already governing BPS Cadence's application across the ecosystem.

## The internal pipeline track

BSA's mandate explicitly includes referring trainees into SUC-incubated startups — for that to be a real mechanism rather than a stated aspiration, part of the curriculum should be calibrated to what those businesses actually need staffing for: bookkeeping, customer service, basic digital marketing execution, virtual assistance. This is the track that makes the internal referral pipeline coherent, not an afterthought to it.
