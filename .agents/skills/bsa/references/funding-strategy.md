# BSA Funding Strategy: A Real Sequence, Not One Ask

BEF is already a registered NGO (Incorporated Trustees under CAMA — see `bef`'s `references/identity-and-framework.md`), which matters directly here: grant eligibility generally requires registered nonprofit status, and BSA inherits BEF's standing rather than needing to establish its own.

## Tier 1 — accessible now, zero track record required

Small, rolling-basis grants explicitly open to new organizations: the AFNet Flexible Grant (roughly $5,000) and programs like Oneness Revival's SEED grants ($25,000–$50,000, structurally named for exactly this stage). This is the realistic starting point for an initiative with nothing built yet — not the large foundation funds further down this list.

## Tier 2 — once a real pilot exists

Early-stage-social-enterprise funders that want to see something operating, not just a plan: Draper Richards Kaplan Foundation funds up to $300,000 specifically for social impact organizations with scalable, sustainable approaches. This tier is reachable once Tier 1 funding has produced one real, running pilot to point to — not before.

## Tier 3 — later, with real outcomes data

Major foundation partnerships at the scale of the Mastercard Foundation's Nigeria work (Young Africa Works strategy, targeting 10 million young Nigerians by 2030, already running close to 20 active partnerships in-country). Most of their programs don't accept unsolicited proposals — realistically reached through an existing partner organization or a demonstrated track record substantial enough to be approached directly, not a cold application at BSA's current stage.

## Internal bootstrap funding comes first, before any external tier

DBI already generates real revenue (DSE entry fees, DFY, SUC equity), and BEF already receives DBI's $1,000-per-completed-NBC-intern reimbursement (`dbi`'s `SKILL.md`). A modest internal allocation to get one genuine pilot running is what turns Tier 1's small grants into Tier 2's larger ones — every funding tier above Tier 1 wants to see something operating first, and "nothing on ground" is specifically what none of them will fund. This should be the actual first move, not external fundraising.

## The corporate-partnership angle specific to BSA's trade-skills lane

Construction firms, telecom operators, and power companies are on public record citing the skilled-trades shortage as a direct constraint on their own operations — a funding pitch to these companies isn't generic CSR appeal, it's addressing a problem they've already stated is theirs. This is a more targeted, credible partnership angle than approaching corporate partners with an unconnected ask.
