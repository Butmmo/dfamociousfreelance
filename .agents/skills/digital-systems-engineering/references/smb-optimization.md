# SMB Optimization System

Local business automation via GoHighLevel (GHL). The most architecturally modern of the seven inside DBI Citadel (TypeScript, modular components) purely because it was the MVP installed into the app first — that's an installation-order fact, not a signal that its content differs in kind from the other six.

## The core method: the 3-Problem Method, anchored on Google Reviews

Every Hot lead gets diagnosed with three connected problems forming a causal chain — **the Gap, the Leak, and the Lift** — framed around the business's Google reviews specifically. Review Automation is the deliberate first system sold, not because it's the only offering, but because a reviews gap is visible, provable, and low-friction to explain in a first conversation; other GHL-built systems expand the relationship afterward.

## Manual-first prospecting, deliberately low-tech to start

The lead-harvest method is explicitly manual, not tool-heavy, on purpose: scout local businesses on Google Maps into a physical notebook first (not a spreadsheet — the friction is intentional, it forces genuine attention to each lead), then transfer to Google Sheets, follow the business into LinkedIn, use ContactOut to find the owner's personal email, check Facebook and Instagram for additional signal, score the lead against the **Grand Slam Rubric**, then enter it into the Notion CRM only once it's qualified. Manual before automated is the whole point — a student who skips straight to automation tools never develops the judgment the scoring rubric depends on.

## Pricing

Priced per-system rather than one flat tier ladder — individual GHL builds (Review Automation, Speed-to-Lead, AI Receptionist, and others) run roughly $197-$497/month each, expanding as the relationship grows past the first sold system.

## The 45-Day Implementation Playbook

Build Your Foundation → Manual Lead Harvest → Outreach Launch → First Close → Deliver and Expand → Scale the Manual Machine → Month 2 Launch. The playbook itself is Notion-based (not Zoho CRM, an earlier version's tooling that's since been fully replaced).

## Why this is the broadest-applicability path of the seven

Unlike the other six, which lock to one specific niche (home care, ministry, ecommerce, podcasts, LinkedIn), SMB's method generalizes across almost any local business category — the 3-Problem Method and Grand Slam Rubric are built to be re-applied to a new niche/geography combination once the first one is proven, which is part of why it's the path most students graduate into productizing at Stage 2 (see the `startup-catalyst` skill's Stage 1 → Stage 2 mapping, where SMB connects to Template Vault, License, and Course).
