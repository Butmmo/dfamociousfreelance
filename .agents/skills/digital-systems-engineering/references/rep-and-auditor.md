# The Rep, the Auditor, and DFY Verification

Inherited from the DFS Constitution (Art. XV-XVIII), carried forward because DFY is a real financial obligation and currently has no named human verifying it.

## The two roles

**The DSE Representative (Rep)** is the direct link between DBI's institutional structure and the individual beneficiary. Core function: direct guidance, metric collection, and performance tracking to help the beneficiary achieve DFY — measured by qualified months. Rep authority is explicitly conditional, renewable, and revocable, and Reps are accountable to executive authority, compliance systems, and performance evaluation.

**This runs on the BPS Cadence, not an ad-hoc schedule** — daily binary activity data reviewed on a weekly reporting cadence, with each qualifying period structured as its own Belief → Affirmation → Evaluation cycle. Applies to every beneficiary a Rep tracks, regardless of whether they came through NBO or paid full price directly — the cadence is a DBI-wide standard, not an NBO-specific one. Full detail: `dfg`'s `references/bps-cadence.md`.

Operational leaders in this line are constitutionally expected to function as **disciplinarians, mentors, and system protectors — not informal supervisors.** The distinction is deliberate: the role carries real accountability, not just encouragement.

**The Auditor** is expressly and operationally separated from the Rep, so that *collecting* financial performance is never the same function as *auditing and verifying* it.

## Why this separation matters more now, not less

DFY obligates a beneficiary to remit 20% of every earning month, and SUC eligibility turns on hitting $450,000 cumulative. Both run on reported income. **Self-reported income verified by nobody is the weakest link in the entire DBI structure** — and it gets weaker, not stronger, as software scales up the volume of collected claims.

The person mentoring a beneficiary toward SUC eligibility must not be the person certifying they hit the threshold. The mentor has every incentive to see their mentee succeed; that's what makes them a good mentor and a bad auditor.

## Rep compensation should be performance-linked, carefully

DFS's original constitution ties Rep compensation directly to the measurable success of their assigned beneficiaries in reaching qualified months and DFY progression, on top of base salary — worth keeping, since it aligns the Rep's incentive with the institution's actual goal (transitioning beneficiaries into earning professionals) rather than just hours logged. **The caution that has to travel with this:** it must not create pressure that contradicts the DFY-period support-not-punishment reframing above. A Rep incentivized by outcomes should be motivated to give genuinely effective support, not to push a struggling beneficiary harder than the acceptable/correctable-failure classification (`bef`'s `references/identity-and-framework.md`) would justify. Structure the incentive around sustained beneficiary progress, not around punishing beneficiaries who are behind.

## Staffing sequence, at realistic scale

1. **Build the clerical/tracking layer into the app first** — qualified-month counting, DFY remittance calculation, submission timestamping. Highest leverage, no salary cost, fully automatable. (Same logic as NBS's clerical mandate — see `nbo`'s `references/training-governance-roles.md`.)
2. **Fill the Auditor role before the Rep role.** Counterintuitive but correct: mentoring is already happening informally through NBO sponsors, while verification is currently being done by nobody. The gap is verification.
3. **Industrial-training interns fit the Rep role well** — supervised metric collection and beneficiary guidance is genuine training, and it matches the constitutional progression path from Beneficiary into operational roles.
4. **Keep the Auditor with salaried staff, not interns.** Independence is the entire point of the role, and an intern hoping to be assimilated into the system is not structurally independent of the people evaluating them.

## Employment status

Rep and Auditor are **employed, salaried roles** (or genuine industrial-training internships), unlike beneficiaries, who hold independent-contractor status. Moving from beneficiary into either role is a change in legal relationship — state it explicitly at the transition.
