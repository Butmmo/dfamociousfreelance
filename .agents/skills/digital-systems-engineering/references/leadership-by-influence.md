# Leadership by Influence: The SUC People-Management Standard

Replaces the earlier Sapphire Director (Neolife rank) requirement for SUC eligibility. Applies universally to any DSE participant seeking SUC entry, regardless of whether they came through NBO's subsidized track or paid full price directly — this is a DBI-owned standard now, not dependent on an external company's compensation plan.

## Why Sapphire Director had to go, precisely

Not because ranks are inherently suspect — because of one specific mechanical fact: Sapphire Director requires maintaining a downline leg and real group volume, with no path to it through personal consumption alone. Gating SUC on that specific rank didn't just correlate with recruitment, it required it, which is exactly the feature *FTC v. Vemma* and *BurnLounge* treat as the marker of a pyramid regardless of how well-designed everything around it is. The replacement needed to test the same underlying capability (real people-management ability) without inheriting that mechanism.

## The B.L.A.Z.E.R. connection

This standard operationalizes **Leadership by Influence** — one of the six B.L.A.Z.E.R. core values (`bef`'s `SKILL.md`): authority derived from demonstrable character and proven competence, not title. Sapphire Director was measuring this with someone else's yardstick. DBI measures it with its own now.

## Two paths, either satisfies the standard

**Path 1 — Verified Mentorship (the default path, available to every DSE participant).** A candidate qualifies once they have personally invited and actively mentored **5 people** through to their own **full DFY completion** — not just onboarding, not just becoming a Next Blazers Beneficiary, but each mentee independently completing their own DFY (12 qualified months or the $18,000 cap, per `references/pricing-and-dfy.md`). The mentee doesn't need to have entered through NBO specifically — inviting someone into DSE at all, then mentoring them through, satisfies this. Verified through NBS records with sign-off from the mentee's own Rep (see "Tracking" below).

**Why 5, and why full completion, not just enrollment:** this is deliberately a high bar, and the height is the safeguard, not just the ambition. Pushing 5 people through onboarding is achievable by volume alone. Getting 5 people to each independently sustain $2,000+ qualified months repeatedly is not something a candidate can coerce — it only happens if the mentorship was genuinely good. The bar itself is the anti-gaming mechanism; no separate policy check is needed to prevent someone from gaming it with warm bodies.

**Why self-sponsorship is safe here where it wasn't safe for Sapphire Director:** the critical distinction, worth stating explicitly because it's the question any future reviewer will ask first. Sapphire Director paid the sponsor an override on downline volume — a direct multi-level compensation flow from mentee to mentor. This standard has no equivalent: DBI keeps 100% of every mentee's DFY remittance regardless of who mentored them. Nothing flows upward. A mentor here earns nothing from a mentee's DFY beyond the qualification credit itself. That absence of an override is what makes "recruit your own mentees" structurally closer to a teacher's outcomes than an MLM upline's economics — the mechanism the FTC's test actually targets simply isn't present.

**Path 2 — Verified Retail (optional, for candidates active in the Blazer People Network).** Documented sales to real, verified non-network customers — not personal consumption, not downline volume — of at least **250 PV per month, sustained for 3 consecutive months.** That figure isn't arbitrary: it's the same 250 PPV already advised as the personal-use ceiling elsewhere in BPN, reused deliberately so this path asks for "sell about as much as you'd use yourself," not a sales quota layered on top of it — consistent with the emphasis on product use and people development, not selling. Three consecutive months, not one strong push, is what makes it a real capability rather than a one-time result. Express this in PV, not a fixed dollar figure — Neolife's SRP/DP pricing varies by market, so a local-currency equivalent should come from DBI's actual regional price list, not an estimate. Worth having on record as the standard this path should eventually be held to as it matures: Herbalife's 2016 FTC settlement required at least two-thirds of rewards to come from verifiable retail sales to genuine end-users, no more than one-third from personal consumption — treat that ratio as the aspirational benchmark for what "optional selling" should look like in practice here.

## Verification: the same split DFY already runs on

Mentorship activity itself is self-reported by the mentor (qualitative — sessions, topics, cadence). Each mentee's DFY progress is independently verified by *that mentee's own Rep* — who is already tracking it for DFY purposes regardless of who mentored them, so this adds no new verification burden, only a new use for data already being collected. This mirrors the Rep/Auditor separation established in `digital-systems-engineering`'s `references/rep-and-auditor.md`: the person invested in a mentee's success is never the person certifying their result.

## Tracking: extending BPS Assignment 7, not building new infrastructure

BPS Assignment 7 (`nbo`'s `references/nbc-curriculum.md`) already logs sponsor check-ins — session count, virtual/physical split, purpose — for NBC's 40-day window. The mentorship tracker for this standard is the same mechanism, extended across the full DFY horizon instead of NBC's short window.

**Sponsor-of-record: established once, by both sides, early.** When a mentee enters DSE, their mentor is named and the pairing confirmed by *both* the mentee and the mentor — not a unilateral claim by whoever wants the credit. This is what actually makes the tag trustworthy: a one-sided self-declaration is trivial to game after the fact, a two-sided confirmation made before the outcome is known isn't. Once confirmed, the pairing is locked, not casually reassignable.

**The one legitimate exception: a mentor genuinely drops off.** If that happens, reassigning the sponsor-of-record tag requires sign-off from the new mentor, the mentee, and their Rep — three-way confirmation, not a quiet edit. This keeps the exception real without reopening the door to gaming.

**Ongoing check-ins run on the BPS Cadence, not an arbitrary quarterly schedule.** A lightweight weekly touchpoint (session happened or didn't — binary, like the rest of the cadence) plus real substance at 40-day checkpoints, mirroring BPS Assignment 7's own pattern rather than inventing a separate rhythm for mentorship specifically. Both parties attesting, not just the mentor self-reporting, is what keeps this an audit trail rather than a diary. A mentor tracking up to 5 mentees this way is real overhead — keep the weekly layer genuinely lightweight (a yes/no, not a full write-up) so the volume stays sustainable. Full cadence detail: `dfg`'s `references/bps-cadence.md`.

**What the 40-day checkpoint actually reviews:** three things, not one. The mentee's own work and patterns (are they actually progressing, and does the pattern look healthy or strained). Light advice where it's useful. And — the part that makes this checkpoint more than a status update — **the Rep or sponsor's own activity in supporting that mentee.** Is the assigned Rep actually engaging, or has the mentee effectively been left to fend for themselves? A mentor is close enough to the mentee's real experience to notice this, in a way institutional records alone might not surface quickly.

## The mentor as a structural shock, not just a status-checker

If a mentor's 40-day review finds the Rep, sponsor, an expected DBI employee, or DBI itself isn't maintaining the activity a mentee's success actually depends on, the mentor reports it — through a defined online channel, not an informal aside. This is the mentor's real institutional function beyond the mentee relationship itself: **a check that catches failure anywhere in the support chain**, not just failure from the mentee.

**DBI's side of this needs to be genuinely fast and genuinely meaningful, not just receivable.** A report with no real response defeats the entire point of building the channel. Proposed standard, consistent with DFG's people-first framing rather than a bureaucratic intake process: acknowledged within 48-72 hours, then classified using the same Acceptable/Correctable/Disciplinary framework already governing everything else in this ecosystem (`bef`'s `references/identity-and-framework.md`) — a Rep going through a genuinely hard stretch gets support, not sanction; a pattern of neglect across multiple mentees is a different matter, and reassigning the mentee to a different Rep is always on the table regardless of which category the Rep's situation falls into, since the mentee's outcome shouldn't wait on how the Rep's case gets resolved. This response framework is proposed, not yet confirmed as policy — the reporting obligation and the mentor's oversight function are the parts actually specified; the exact response mechanics are my own reasoned addition.

## Mentee-mentor matching: a marketplace, with a real failsafe

Available (unmatched) mentees are listed in an online system; mentors pitch for the ones they want to take on — a marketplace shape, not a price auction, since no payment flows from mentee to mentor (there's no override to bid against, consistent with why self-sponsorship is safe here in the first place). A mentee chooses among interested mentors using **real, verified track record** — the same completion data already tracked for the Verified Mentorship path above, not self-reported claims, so the choice is informed rather than a popularity contest.

**No mentee goes more than 30 days unmatched.** Past that window, the system auto-assigns — weighted toward mentors with stronger verified track records, but genuinely random within that weighting, not deterministic. A pure "always assign the top mentor" rule would concentrate every unclaimed mentee onto whoever's currently best, which both overloads that mentor and starves everyone else of the chance to build a track record in the first place; weighted-random avoids that while still tilting outcomes toward quality.

**One addition worth making explicit, since nothing in the design otherwise limits it:** a soft cap on how many mentees a single mentor carries at once. The 40-day-checkpoint-plus-weekly-touchpoint workload was already built assuming something in the range of 5 active mentees per mentor (`Ongoing check-ins` above) — an unbounded marketplace, or auto-assignment with no ceiling, could push a strong mentor's real load well past what the check-in cadence was actually designed to sustain. This isn't specified in the original design; flagging it as a real gap worth deciding on rather than leaving implicit.

**If a mentee doesn't complete DFY**, no credit toward the mentor's 5 — but the check-in history stays on record regardless. That history has real value beyond credit-counting: a mentor with a consistent pattern of mentees who complete is a different signal than one whose mentees repeatedly drop, and that pattern is only visible if incomplete relationships are kept on file, not discarded.

**Timing matters here, and it's worth flagging early rather than discovering late:** 5 full DFY completions is a long clock — a single beneficiary's own DFY can stretch past a calendar year once non-consecutive qualifying months are allowed for. A candidate who waits until their own DFY is done before starting to mentor risks this running longer than the $450,000/3-5-year SUC window it's meant to sit inside. Mentorship should start as early as day one of a candidate's own DSE journey, running in parallel with their own progress — not treated as something to pick up once they're already earning well themselves. Whatever system tracks a candidate's own DFY progress should surface their mentorship count alongside it, so this doesn't become a surprise discovered late.

**Why this doesn't need more anti-gaming machinery than this:** the outcome bar itself is still the primary safeguard, not the paperwork around it. Two-sided attestation and Rep-verified completion prevent false credit-claiming; they don't need to additionally prevent someone from *actually* mentoring five people well, because that's the entire point.
