# The Ascent

High-ticket sales closing. Zero-capital, remote — no niche-specific tooling to learn, since the product being sold belongs to whoever hires the student as a closer. The most gamified and structurally distinct of the seven: a scored rank system, a 45-day structured bootcamp, and its own direct-scouting engine, rather than the shared "niche cards + Notion CRM" pattern the other five (minus SMB) use.

## The six niche lanes, scored

Each lane is scored on entry difficulty, deal size, competition, durability, safety, and fit — not just listed. Real commission economics, not estimates:

| Lane | Typical deal size | Commission |
|---|---|---|
| Credit & Business Funding | $3,000-$15,000, or % of capital raised | 10-15% |
| Dating & Relationship Coaching | $2,000-$10,000 | 5-20% (10% norm) |
| Real Estate | $5,000-$25,000, or % of capital raised | 10-15% |
| Content & Personal Branding | $2,000-$8,000, sometimes recurring | 5-15% |
| Trading & Trading Bots | $1,500-$10,000+ | 10-20% |
| AI & B2B AI Implementation | $5,000-$50,000+ (largest average ticket) | 10-20% |

Confirm in writing whether a Funding-lane role touches licensed lending activity before accepting it — this is flagged as a real compliance question, not a formality.

## The CLOSER framework

Six stages, the letters spelling the name: **C**larify (ask why they're really here today, let them answer fully before responding), **L**abel (reflect their problem back as a simple label, let them confirm or correct it in their own words — don't assume you've got it right). The remaining stages continue the same call-flow logic through to close; the full breakdown lives in the source system for direct reference.

Two named tools sit alongside the framework: the **Three Freedoms Questions** (push past a prospect's surface answer with repeated "why" and "what can that create for you" to find their real motivation) and the **Universal Objection Method** for handling pushback in the moment.

## The 45-day arc, in three phases

1. **Foundation & First Contact (Days 1-15)** — orientation, learn the CLOSER framework, lock in a niche lane, run all three scout methods, build two proof assets (a self-recorded pitch video and a self-recorded mock call, self-scored against a rubric), build a warm prospect list, send the first outreach wave.
2. **Sustained Scouting & Pipeline (Days 16-30)** — repeated scouting sprints, outreach waves, warm-prospect engagement, mock-call practice, live auditions, pipeline maintenance.
3. **Conversion & Launch (Days 31-45)** — negotiation, vetting offers received, second-round auditions, locking a role, onboarding, first live rep.

## The direct-scouting engine — three methods, not job boards

The system explicitly routes around marketplaces: **Search Scouting** (open-web search patterns to find companies before they ever post a job), **YouTube Mining** (review niche-relevant channels for visible application/booking pages and team-size signals), and **Track Active Closers** (find people already working as closers or setters in the niche — every company they mention is a validated target). A student is expected to log 25+ tiered companies before moving to outreach.

## Proof assets: self-made, not free work for a real prospect

Both proof assets — the pitch video and the mock call — are self-directed practice, recorded solo or with a practice partner, never performed for an actual prospect. This is consistent with the cross-cutting rule in `SKILL.md`: build proof on self-made material, never give away real closing work for free to win a role. A student's positioning statement and resume are built from this practice, not from a free trial period worked for an employer.

## Progression: seven ranks

Recruit (0) → Scout (10) → Prospector (22) → Apprentice Closer (38) → Field Closer (58) → Contract Closer (78) → Certified High-Ticket Closer (95). Thresholds are cumulative XP earned through the 45-day arc's daily tasks, not time served.
