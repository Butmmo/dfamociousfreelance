# Digital Systems Engineering (DSE)

Stage 1 of DBI (a subsidiary of D'Famocious Group). Seven service-based, client-acquisition systems — a student picks **one** and runs it to build cash flow and proof, advancing to Start-up Catalyst (SUC, Stage 2) on outcome-based criteria, not a fixed timeline — see the `startup-catalyst` skill for that stage. Each system targets a distinct market by design, so students across the seven don't compete with each other.

**Working reference: DSE Constitution (Restated).** This skill implements and elaborates that document operationally; where the two differ, the Constitution governs.

## Economics

DSE entry: $900 one-time (or $90 via BEF's NBO subsidy program). Once a beneficiary starts earning, DFY (D'Famocious Year) applies: 20% of every earning month goes back to DBI, for the duration of one qualified year — 12 qualified months (each requiring at least $2,000 net income before the cut), not necessarily consecutive; sub-threshold months still owe the 20%, they just don't advance the count, which is deliberate — it builds urgency and consistency. **DFY concludes at 12 qualified months or $18,000 total remitted, whichever comes first** — a strong performer who hits the cap early has already demonstrated the earning capacity DFY exists to build, so early graduation rewards exactly what the system is designed to produce. Completing DFY (either way) earns the Vetted Digital Systems Engineer (V. DsE.) certification. Full mechanics, the disclosure a beneficiary should receive before DFY starts, and a worked example: `references/pricing-and-dfy.md`.

DSE also determines SUC readiness — outcome-based, not simply time-based, and not financial alone: a beneficiary becomes eligible for a SUC invitation once DSE has produced $450,000 in cumulative earnings within 3-5 years **and** satisfies the Leadership by Influence standard — 5 people personally mentored through their own full DFY completion, or verified retail sales for candidates active in the Blazer People Network. Full mechanics, and why this replaced an earlier MLM-rank requirement: `references/leadership-by-influence.md`. If invited, SUC entry is $2,000 (DBI's standard 25% equity applies). Skipping DSE entirely and entering SUC directly costs $6,000 (same 25% equity) or $24,000 (DBI's equity reduced to 10% instead) — a deliberate ladder, not a cliff: every direct-entry tier keeps DBI genuinely aligned with the business, rather than a top tier that removes DBI's stake entirely. Full reasoning for why this ladder replaced an earlier no-equity option: `references/pricing-and-dfy.md`.

BEF is separately developing a business-funding initiative for SUC-stage businesses — no structure, plan, or name exists yet. Don't describe or invent mechanics for this until it's actually defined.

## Who verifies DFY

DFY and the $450,000 SUC threshold both run on reported income, which makes verification structural, not administrative — and the same separation now governs the Leadership by Influence standard too. Two deliberately separated roles handle it: the **Rep** (guidance, metric collection, performance tracking toward qualified months) and the **Auditor** (verification) — kept apart so the person mentoring a beneficiary toward eligibility is never the person certifying they reached it. Both are employed/salaried roles, unlike beneficiaries, who hold independent-contractor status; crossing from one to the other is a change in legal relationship. Full detail and staffing sequence: `references/rep-and-auditor.md`.

## Two cross-cutting rules, true across all seven

**Prove skill on self-made material, never on a real prospect's time for free.** Every system that needs a portfolio before the first real client teaches the same move: build the proof on something the student made themselves — a dummy Shopify store, practice podcast episodes, ghostwritten posts for a fictional persona, a self-recorded mock sales call. That's real, honest practice, and nobody experiences a "free job" because there's no real customer on the other end of it. What doesn't belong in this skill: offering free work directly to an actual prospect to win them over — a free sample post, a free week of service, a free flow built for a real store. Free work reads as common and trains a prospect to expect it, which undercuts the paid close it was meant to earn. The one exception is a customer relationship that already carries real trust — an existing warm contact, not a cold prospect — where the calculus is genuinely different.

**Worked example of applying this, not just stating it:** CareBridge's underlying system offers a fully free pilot to cold, mystery-shopper-researched agencies — a real instance of the pattern to avoid, corrected in `references/carebridge.md` into a small, guarantee-backed paid trial instead, which keeps the same proof-before-commitment logic without the free-work downside. The Authority Engine has the same problem in a different shape — an entire step built around writing a free sample post for a real cold prospect — corrected in `references/authority-engine.md` by leaning on the already-legitimate dummy portfolio instead, with a small paid (not free) diagnostic post as the bridge for a genuinely interested prospect. Ministry Systems needed no correction at all: its pilot runs on the student's own parish first, then expands through warm network and referrals, never cold prospects — see `references/ministry-systems.md` for why that's the trust exception working as intended, not an oversight.

**The niche-scoring shape.** Five of the seven systems structure their target-market data the same way: a short list of segments, each with *why this segment* (the underlying demand reason) and *find* (concretely how to locate real prospects in it) — not a generic TAM stat. The Ascent goes further, scoring each lane on entry difficulty, deal size, competition, durability, safety, and fit. Use this shape whenever helping a student evaluate or narrow a niche, inside whichever system they're in.

## Choosing among the seven

Point a student toward whichever system's *mechanism* matches temperament, not whichever sounds most lucrative on paper:

- **Live, real-time selling** (calls, objection handling, negotiation) → The Ascent
- **Operations/automation for an emotionally sensitive, relationship-driven niche** → CareBridge or Digital Ministry Systems
- **Marketing/retention systems for ecommerce** → Revenue Recovery Engine
- **General local-business automation, broadest applicability** → SMB Optimization System
- **Audio/video production and editing** → The Broadcast Engine
- **Writing, ghostwriting, personal-brand-building** → The Authority Engine

If nothing stands out, default to whichever system's proof-of-skill exercise (see each system's file) sounds like something the student would actually enjoy doing dozens of times in practice — that's a better predictor of 1-3 year follow-through than projected income.
